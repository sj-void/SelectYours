# Columbia University

Columbia University in the City of New York is a private nonprofit research university in Manhattan, New York [1][16]. This profile covers the two undergraduate schools that admit first-year international applicants together: Columbia College and Columbia Engineering (the Fu Foundation School of Engineering and Applied Science). They share one application, one admissions office, one Common Data Set and one aid office [1][2].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

**Note on sources.** Columbia's admissions, financial aid and career pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches. Facts from those pages were read from search-engine extracts of the live pages (marked "search extract" in Sources) and should be re-checked on the page itself. The Common Data Set PDF was downloaded and read directly.

**Which numbers are combined.** Unless stated, figures are for Columbia College and Columbia Engineering together (CDS "College/Engineering" edition). A separate CDS covers the School of General Studies (GS), a school for non-traditional students, which is not covered here [1][9]. Barnard College has its own profile.

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-aware, full need, no loans.** International applicants must ask for aid when they apply. Those who don't cannot apply for aid at any time during enrollment, unless their citizenship status changes. Columbia says it meets 100% of demonstrated need for admitted international students, and its aid pages say loans are not part of any package; need-based aid is grants and work-study [2][3].
- **Aid is real but limited in number.** In Fall 2025, 248 degree-seeking undergraduate nonresidents received institutional aid, with an average award of $92,699 and a total of $22,989,258 (CDS 2025–26 H6) [1]. That is about 21% of the 1,176 international degree-seeking undergraduates (our calculation) [1].
- **Very selective for internationals.** In Fall 2025, 407 of 15,380 international applicants were admitted (2.6%, our calculation) and 275 enrolled (CDS C1) [1].
- **Testing is optional for Fall 2027,** then required. There are no minimum scores [5].
- **High English minimums.** Columbia College's FAQ lists a minimum of 105 on the TOEFL iBT, 7.5 on IELTS Academic, or 135 on Duolingo, unless English is your home language or the language of instruction at your school. Other English tests are not accepted [5].
- **No evaluative interview.** Interviews are not part of admissions. International applicants may send an InitialView or Vericant video if they wish [7].
- **Insurance.** International students are enrolled in the Columbia student health plan by default ($5,823 for 2026–27), and it is not included in the cost of attendance figure [10][4].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Enrollment:** 6,872 degree-seeking undergraduates in Columbia College and Columbia Engineering (CDS B1); 26,101 graduate students; 35,386 in total [1]. The undergraduate-only student–faculty ratio is 6 to 1 [1].
- **Schools:** Columbia College (liberal arts) and Columbia Engineering. Engineering students were 24% of the Class of 2030 by intended field (Class of 2030 profile, via search extract) [8].
- **Calendar:** Fall and spring semesters (the Engineering catalog charges tuition per term) [4].
- **Founded and campus size:** Founded in 1754 as King's College, it later moved to Morningside Heights. Current campus size wasn't published on the pages checked [32].
- **Nearest airport:** LaGuardia is the closest airport. The M60 Select Bus Service runs from LaGuardia to campus ($3); a taxi is about $45 before tip. There's no airport shuttle; Newark and JFK connect by train or bus [31].
- **Enrollment growth:** Columbia has announced a larger first-year class for Fall 2026 (about 180 students above plan, per the student newspaper) [9].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-aware for international applicants. Columbia meets 100% of demonstrated need for admitted international first-years and transfers seeking a first degree, for all four years [2][3]. The CDS lists institutional need-based aid as available to nonresidents (H6) [1].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - CSS Profile, Columbia code 2116. For the 2027–28 aid year the Profile opened Oct 1, 2026 [3].
  - Parent income documentation, sent through the College Board IDOC service [3]. The CDS lists "parent and student income tax forms" for nonresident first-years (H7) [1].
  - Aid deadlines: ED, Nov 15; RD, Feb 15, 2027 [6].
  - Low-income international students can ask ugrad-finaid@columbia.edu about a CSS Profile fee waiver [3].
  - The CDS (H7) ticks the CSS Profile for nonresidents; ISFAA isn't mentioned on the pages checked (columbia.edu, searched 2026-11-23) [1][3].
- **What a package contains:** Grants and work-study, no loans, paid for from institutional funds [2][3]. Columbia offers exemptions from the academic-year and summer work expectations for study abroad, research, internships and community service, and aid recipients, including internationals, can apply for them; students who study abroad get the work-study replaced by grant [3]. The exact student contribution amount wasn't published on the pages checked.
- **Actual awards (CDS H6, Fall 2025):** 248 aided; average $92,699; total $22,989,258 [1]. An older aid page gives an average international award of $79,375; the CDS figure is the newer one [3].
- **Rules:**
  - Late aid: not possible. Apply at admission or never (unless citizenship changes) [2].
  - Outside scholarships: allowed, but the pages read do not explain how they change a package [3].
  - Transfers: aid is available for international transfers seeking a first degree [2][3]. Our CSV records the same package for College and Engineering.
  - Renewal: you reapply every year; Columbia sends instructions in early March, and the deadline is in early May. Aid continues for four years while need continues [23].
  - International aid can't be awarded in a later year if it wasn't awarded at admission, except after a severe, unforeseen change, reviewed case by case [23].
- **Parent contribution thresholds:** Columbia pages disagree on the family income below which parents contribute nothing: $60,000 in an older FAQ and $66,000 on another page. Check the current figure [3].
- **Merit scholarships:** none listed; Columbia's pages describe need-based aid only [3].
- **Net price calculator:** https://undergrad.admissions.columbia.edu/affordability/calculator (page listed in search results; whether it supports international families isn't stated on the pages checked) [4].

**Consistency with our CSV:** No contradiction. The CSV's verdict (need-aware, full need, no loans, apply at admission) matches the pages above [2][3].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App or Coalition App on Scoir, with no preference; QuestBridge for finalists [7]. QuestBridge is listed as an accepted route; QuestBridge's National College Match is open only to students at US high schools (any citizenship) and to US citizens and permanent residents abroad [24].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [6]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Aid forms by | Decision |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Early Decision | Nov 1, 2026 | Nov 15, 2026 | On or before Dec 15, 2026 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 1, 2027 (11:59 p.m. local time) | Feb 15, 2027 | On or before Apr 15, 2027 |

  The CDS gives the same ED closing date (Nov 1) and notification date (Dec 15) for Fall 2025 [1].
- **Fee and fee waivers:** $85, waivable for applicants with financial need (CDS 2025–26 C13) [1].
- **Required parts:** The Common App or Coalition essay; six short Columbia-specific answers of up to 150 words each; a counselor recommendation with transcript, school profile and mid-year report; and two teacher recommendations from academic subjects. Extra recommendations are discouraged [7]. ED applicants sign an ED agreement [6].
- **English proficiency:** Minimums are TOEFL iBT 105 (report code 2116), IELTS Academic 7.5, or Duolingo 135. Students can also qualify through SAT or ACT section scores, or if English is the home language or the language of instruction. Columbia accepts no other English test [5].
- **International documents:** Not published on the pages checked (columbia.edu, searched 2026-11-23). Optional InitialView or Vericant videos are accepted [7].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** More than 125 majors and minors in Columbia College [13]. Up to three programs of study per student [13].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 27%, computer and information sciences 16%, engineering 12%, biological and biomedical sciences 7%, English 5% [30].
- **Curriculum:** Core curriculum. College students take Literature Humanities, Contemporary Civilization, Art Humanities, Music Humanities, Frontiers of Science, University Writing and two Global Core courses, plus language, science and physical education. The Core is one third to one half of a College student's courses and about one fourth for Engineering students [13].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 11 to 1 for undergraduates and graduates together, 6 to 1 undergraduate-only (CDS I2) [1]. In Fall 2025, 2,113 undergraduate class sections: 524 had 2–9 students, 704 had 10–19, 429 had 20–29, 86 had 30–39, 69 had 40–49, 200 had 50–99 and 101 had 100 or more (CDS I3). About 58% had fewer than 20 students (our calculation) [1].
- **Fields:** Columbia Engineering offers biomedical, chemical, civil, computer, mechanical and other engineering plus computer science [13]. A 3-2 Combined Plan lets students from partner colleges join Engineering (208 transfer applicants in Fall 2025) [1].
- **Opportunities:** Undergraduate research, study abroad, cross-registration: Not published on the pages checked (columbia.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **STEM majors:** Columbia doesn't publish an undergraduate list. Check the CIP code next to "Program Major" on page 1 of your I-20 against the federal STEM list. STEM OPT needs a job of at least 20 hours a week with an E-Verify employer, and ISSO lists a $650 STEM OPT administration fee [25].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 98% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,454 of 1,480 Fall 2024 entrants still enrolled) [1]. Six-year graduation rate 96% for the Fall 2019 cohort (1,331 of 1,392). Four-year rate 84.4% (1,175 of 1,392, our calculation) (CDS B4–B21) [1].
- **After graduation:** Class of 2025 Beyond Columbia Survey, College and Engineering: 1,745 degree earners and outcomes for 68.7% (1,199). The most common job functions were web and software engineering (19.3%), finance (19.0%) and consulting (9.6%). Columbia is the most popular graduate-school destination [14]. Admissions pages cite 87% employed or in graduate school within six months and a $97,303 average starting salary [14].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings are $109,430 ten years after entry and $87,082 six years after entry. They cover federal-aid recipients only and exclude international students [16].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 1,176 degree-seeking international undergraduates in College and Engineering (CDS B2) [1]. The Class of 2029 profile reports 16% international students from 83 countries; Class of 2028, 17% and 93 countries [8]. The Class of 2030 admitted group represents 111 countries [8].
- **Support:** The International Students and Scholars Office (ISSO) handles I-20s, travel signatures, work authorization and OPT [11]. Orientation details and the I-20 issuing process: Not published on the pages checked (columbia.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Work:**
  - CPT is limited to internships required by the degree; elective internships don't qualify [11].
  - Post-completion OPT can be requested up to 90 days before the program end date; the I-765 must be filed within 30 days of the OPT I-20 [11].
  - STEM OPT extension needs a paid job of at least 20 hours a week with an E-Verify employer, an online course, and a $650 ISSO fee [11].
  - On-campus work: up to 20 hours a week in term, full time in official vacations. It can start in your first semester and needs no OPT authorization. Paid off-campus work needs OPT, generally after two full-time semesters [26].
- **Practicalities:**
  - Break housing: residence halls stay open over winter break for students housed in both fall and spring. Students studying abroad in spring, withdrawing or graduating in February aren't eligible [27].
  - Hardship: Columbia has an International Student Hardship Fund [28].
  - Mentor programs and student organizations: Not published on the pages checked.

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** On-campus housing is guaranteed for four years for College and Engineering undergraduates, subject to keeping eligibility. 3-2 Combined Plan students are guaranteed only the first year [12]. CDS F1: 99% of first-years and 90% of all undergraduates live in college housing [1]. The housing page gives 94% of all undergraduates and 99% of first-years [12].
- **Location:** Manhattan, a large city. Nearest airport: Not published on the pages checked (columbia.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at New York Cntrl Pk Twr, NY, 3.3 km from campus: average temperature 55.8°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 30.4°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 82.6°F; 49.52 inches of precipitation a year, including 29.8 inches of snowfall [20].
- **Safety:** The 2026 Annual Security and Fire Safety Report was published on Sep 7, 2026: https://publicsafety.columbia.edu/annualsecurityreport. Morningside campus, 2025 (on campus): 121 burglaries (120 in student housing), 16 rapes and 10 fondling reports. Public property adjacent to campus had 26 motor-vehicle thefts and 3 robberies in 2025 [29]. Federal data: https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety [15].
- **Student body:** CDS B1 first-years: 863 full-time men and 934 women; degree-seeking College/Engineering undergraduates: 4,365 men and 4,299 women full-time [1]. College Scorecard: 50.2% men, 49.8% women [16]. 81% of first-years are from out of state; 8% of men join fraternities and 16% of women sororities (CDS F1) [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** All full-time domestic students and all international students are enrolled automatically in the Columbia student health plan. Waivers require comparable ACA-compliant US coverage plus proof of medical evacuation and repatriation coverage for visa holders; approval is not guaranteed. The fall 2026 waiver window ran July 15 to Sept 30, 2026; the spring window is Dec 15, 2026 to Feb 15, 2027 for new spring students [10].
- **Cost:** $5,823 for Aug 15, 2026 to Aug 14, 2027 [10]. Waivers must be renewed every year [10].
- **Services:**
  - Counseling and Psychological Services (CPS): short-term individual and couples counseling, support groups and medication consultations for Morningside undergraduates who have paid the health service fee. Book on the patient portal or call 212-854-2878 [18].
  - Disability Services: register to get accommodations (212-854-2388); available whatever your health-fee level [19].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Distinctive features:** the undergraduate Core Curriculum and the New York City location [13].
- **US presidents:** Barack Obama (Columbia College 1983). Theodore and Franklin D. Roosevelt studied at Columbia Law School and were awarded posthumous degrees in 2008. Dwight D. Eisenhower was Columbia's president (1948–53) before becoming US president [17].
- **International:** Toomas Hendrik Ilves (Columbia College '76), fourth President of Estonia [17].
