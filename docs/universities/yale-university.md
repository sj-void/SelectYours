# Yale University

Yale is a private university in New Haven, Connecticut. Its undergraduate school is Yale College, which has 14 residential colleges and more than 80 majors [11][19].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind for everyone, full need, no loans.** "A family's ability to pay is not a factor in the admissions process—for any student, anywhere in the world." Aid offers "meet 100% of demonstrated need" regardless of citizenship or immigration status and are made in scholarships, not loans [2][3].
- **Apply for aid at admission.** Check the aid box on the Common App, Coalition or QuestBridge application; the aid office then contacts you [13]. International aid is set at admission and renews automatically unless the number of children in undergraduate study changes; families may reapply if finances change [14].
- **Aid forms.** The CSS Profile (code 3987) is required of all aid applicants. Yale does not accept the ISFAA in place of its own forms. International Early Action aid documents are due Dec 1, 2026; Regular Decision Feb 15, 2027 [2][13].
- **SAT or ACT required for everyone.** Scores from other exams, including international leaving exams, do not meet the requirement. AP and IB scores are optional. Applicants without two or more years at an English-medium school must also send an English test [4][5][9].
- **Student share.** The standard student share is $3,700 a year, and outside scholarships first reduce it before reducing Yale scholarship [14]. Yale aid "may have U.S. tax implications" for international students [8].
- **Aid actually given.** CDS 2025–26 H6: 459 nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid, averaging $84,185 (total $38,640,976) [1].
- **Very selective.** 4.7% admitted in Fall 2025 (CDS C1) [1]. Yale does not publish international applicant or admit counts in the CDS (the residency table is blank).

## 1. Snapshot

- **Enrollment:** 6,667 undergraduates and 15,657 students in all, counted Oct 2025 (CDS 2025–26 B1) [1]. Full-time degree-seeking undergraduates: 3,239 men, 3,318 women, 76 unknown; plus 3 part-time degree-seekers [1]. Graduate and professional: 8,990 [1].
- **Residential system:** every undergraduate is randomly assigned to one of 14 residential colleges [11].
- **Founded:** 1701 [22].
- **Campus:** 261-acre central campus in New Haven; 1,108 acres in total, including West Campus, athletic fields, a golf course and a nature preserve (2021 fact sheet; acreage figures vary between Yale sheets) [22].
- **Calendar:** fall and spring terms. Residential colleges close for winter recess, from noon on December 23 (Fall 2026) until January 15, 2027 [23].
- **Nearest airport:** Tweed New Haven (HVN) is about four miles from campus but has few flights. Larger airports (JFK, LaGuardia, Bradley) connect by GO Airport Shuttle or Connecticut Limousine, and Newark by Amtrak to New Haven Union Station [34].
- **Student–faculty ratio:** 8.8 to 1 (CDS I2) [1].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-blind for all applicants worldwide; meets 100% of demonstrated need regardless of citizenship or immigration status; no loans in initial offers (families may choose them) [2][3].
- **Family payments (typical assets):** under $100,000 income: $0, with aid covering tuition, fees, housing, food, travel, hospitalization insurance, plus a $2,000 first-year start-up grant. About $10,000 at $150,000; $20,000 at $200,000; $43,000 at $250,000 [3][8]. The calculators are designed for parents living and working in the US and "won't give accurate estimates" for international students [8].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - Tick the aid box on the application, then the aid office contacts you with required documents [13].
  - CSS Profile, code 3987. Yale does not accept the ISFAA instead of its own forms [2][13].
  - Documents go through IDOC after the CSS Profile. For 2027–28 Yale asks for 2025 income documents. The pages read do not detail non-US tax documents or translation rules for international families [13].
  - Deadlines: QuestBridge Nov 1, 2026; Early Action Nov 1 (US) and Dec 1 (international), 2026; Regular Decision Feb 15, 2027; transfer Apr 1, 2027 [13].
- **What a package contains:** Yale scholarship plus a standard student share of $3,700 a year [14]. For zero-parent-share offers students are expected to cover about $3,700 for books and personal expenses [8]. The pages read do not describe a summer earnings expectation (a "Summer Aid" page exists, but its terms weren't published in the pages checked) [14]. International aid offers include allowances for housing, meals and travel to and from campus twice a year, and account for differences between the US economy and the home country's [10].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 459 nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid, average $84,185, total $38,640,976. Against 706 nonresident undergraduates, about 65% (our calculation; CDS aid year and headcount year may differ) [1]. Yale also reports an average need-based scholarship of $75,220 across all students [3].
- **Rules:**
  - Renewal: International students apply once, at admission; aid renews automatically unless the number of children in undergraduate programs changes. US citizens and permanent residents reapply every year [14]. (The international page also says enrolled students do not need to reapply each year [10].)
  - Outside scholarships reduce the $3,700 student share first, then Yale scholarship dollar for dollar (or fund a one-time technology purchase of up to $2,500) [14].
  - Late applications: families whose aid application is incomplete at decisions are still eligible, and offers are not reduced for late submission [13].
  - Transfers: the aid deadline is April 1, 2027. A transfer-specific aid rule for internationals wasn't published on the pages checked [13].
  - Applying for aid after admission: not stated on the pages read, beyond the late-application rule above.
- **Merit scholarships:** Not mentioned; all aid described is need-based [2][3].
- **Net price calculator:** College Board NPC, https://npc.collegeboard.org/app/yale, and other tools at https://finaid.yale.edu/estimate; not accurate for international families [8].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App, Coalition Application on SCOIR or QuestBridge Application; one application per cycle [6]. QuestBridge National College Match deadline Nov 1; whether internationals may use QuestBridge was not stated on the pages read [6][7].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027:** Single-Choice Early Action Nov 1, 2026 (decision mid-December, reply May 1); QuestBridge Match Nov 1 (early December result); Regular Decision Jan 2, 2027 (decision late March, reply May 1) [6][7].
- **Fee and waivers:** $80 application fee (CDS 2025–26 C13) [1]. Yale issues no waiver of its own; request one through the Common App or Coalition if you qualify; QuestBridge is free [9].
- **Required parts:** the platform application and essays (topics listed at https://admissions.yale.edu/essay-topics); two teacher recommendations from core academic subjects and one counselor letter; school report with transcript; Mid-Year Report with first-marking-period senior grades; self-reported SAT or ACT. Optional supplements for art, dance, music, film or STEM research [9].
- **English proficiency:** Required if you have not spent at least two years at a school where English is the language of instruction. Accepted: TOEFL iBT (about 5 or higher on the new scale; 100 or higher on the old), IELTS 7 or higher, Cambridge English 185 or higher, Duolingo 120 or higher, or InitialView (live video, no score) [5][10].
- **International documents:** All materials must be in English. Non-English documents need a certified translation; self-prepared translations are not accepted. Applicants from schools outside the US complete the same requirements as everyone else [10]. National exam results do not meet the test requirement [10].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** More than 80 majors, with 35+ certificate programs; about 20% of students complete a double major [19]. There is no core curriculum: students take 36 courses, start undeclared and choose a major by the end of sophomore year [19]. Most popular fields (CDS 2025–26 J1, bachelor's degrees): social sciences 23.0%, biological and life sciences 9.9%, interdisciplinary studies 9.6%, computer and information sciences 8.5%, mathematics and statistics 8.4%, history 6.4%, engineering 4.8% [1].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 23%, biological and biomedical sciences 11%, interdisciplinary studies 11%, computer and information sciences 9%, mathematics and statistics 8% [33].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 8.8 to 1 (CDS I2: 11,376 students, 1,299 faculty) [1]. Fall 2025 undergraduate class sections (I3): 376 with 2–9 students, 599 with 10–19, 130 with 20–29, 64 with 30–39, 39 with 40–49, 79 with 50–99, 46 with 100+ (1,333 in total) [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering majors include Applied Physics and Biomedical, Chemical, Electrical, Environmental and Mechanical Engineering; computer science and joint CS majors are offered [19]. Pre-health and pre-law advising: Not published on the pages checked (yale.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Opportunities:** Nearly 20 departments offer simultaneous 4-year bachelor's and master's pathways; 5-year bachelor's and master's programs exist with several Yale schools [19]. Study abroad and research: 23% of the Class of 2024 studied abroad (47% of the pre-COVID Class of 2019) [35]. Cross-registration and honors: Not published on the pages checked (yale.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **STEM majors:** Yale publishes no list on the pages checked. STEM OPT eligibility depends on the CIP code on your I-20 matching the federal STEM list; ask OISS.

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 99.4% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,545 of 1,554 Fall 2024 entrants) [1]. Fall 2019 cohort: 52.8% graduated within 4 years (818 of 1,550) and 96.3% within 6 years (1,493 of 1,550) (CDS B4–B11, our calculation). The low 4-year figure for this cohort probably reflects pandemic-era leaves of absence (our inference) [1]. Yale says 88% of Yale College graduates have no student loan debt [3].
- **After graduation (Class of 2025, Office of Career Strategy, six months after graduating):**
  - About 95.3% were employed or in graduate school, with a 90.7% knowledge rate.
  - Mean starting salary: $94,028 for those employed full time in the US.
  - Top industries: financial services, education, technology, consulting and healthcare [27].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard data could not be retrieved. Scorecard figures would cover only federal-aid recipients and exclude international students.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 706 degree-seeking nonresident undergraduates (10.6%) and 155 nonresident first-years (9.5% of 1,633) (CDS B2) [1]. Yale says students come from "over 100 countries" [11].
- **Support:** The Office of International Students & Scholars (OISS), 421 Temple Street, New Haven, handles immigration, travel and visas, and employment and taxes [17]. Its page lists federal-court rulings from mid-November 2026 about CPT guidance and the duration-of-status rule, so check OISS for current rules [17]. I-20 process and orientation details: Not published on the pages checked (yale.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Work:** On-campus work requires full-time enrollment. CPT is for work integral to the curriculum. OPT is available (timing not read). Off-campus work for economic hardship is possible in some cases [18]. Hours limits and STEM OPT details: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Break housing:** None. All undergraduate residences close for winter recess (December 23, 2026 to January 15, 2027), so plan travel or off-campus housing [23][24]. Yale College regulations also bar students on a term or year abroad from student housing [24].
- **Organizations:** 500+ student organizations and 8 cultural and resource centers [11].
- **Mentor programs:** Not published on the pages checked (yale.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** Every undergraduate is assigned to one of 14 residential colleges [11]. CDS 2025–26 F1: 100% of first-years and 77% of undergraduates live in college-owned or affiliated housing; 91% of first-years and 92% of undergraduates are from out of state (international students excluded) [1]. A four-year housing guarantee was not stated on the pages read.
- **Location:** New Haven, Connecticut [12]. Transport: Not published on the pages checked (yale.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at New Haven Tweed Airport, CT, 6.2 km from campus: average temperature 52.0°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 25.2°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 79.8°F; 41.79 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [28].
- **Safety:** Yale's Annual Security Report page moved (HTTP 404 at its redirect target), and the current report wasn't found on the pages checked (yale.edu, searched 2026-11-23). Federal data: https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety.
- **Student body:** CDS B1: full-time degree-seeking undergraduates 3,239 men, 3,318 women, 76 of unknown sex [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** Yale requires students enrolled at least half-time in a degree program to have health insurance, through Yale Health or a comparable alternative. Waivers need an ACA-compliant plan. Waived students keep Basic Student Health Services (not insurance) [16]. The 2026–27 premium and any international-student rule weren't published on the pages checked.
- **Services:**
  - Yale Health Mental Health & Counseling: free, with no referral needed, for students enrolled at least half-time, even if they waived Yale's hospitalization coverage. Mostly short-term therapy, with no fixed visit limit [25].
  - Urgent help: an on-call clinician 24/7 on (203) 432-0290; after hours, Acute Care on (203) 432-0123 [25].
  - Peer support: Walden Peer Counseling for undergraduates [25].
  - Disability: Student Accessibility Services (https://sas.yale.edu) [32].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Affordability:** 88% of Yale College graduates have no student loan debt, and the average annual need-based scholarship is $75,220 [3].
- **US presidents:** five alumni have held the office: William Howard Taft, Gerald Ford, George H. W. Bush, Bill Clinton and George W. Bush [20].
- **Global leaders:** the Jackson School says Yale graduates have included presidents or prime ministers of Mexico, South Korea, Germany and Iceland [21].
- **Nobel laureates:** Yale's pages disagree. The Jackson School cites 27 alumni laureates; another Yale page counts 65 alumni or faculty [21][20]. We don't give a single figure.
