# University of Notre Dame

The University of Notre Dame is a private Catholic research university at Notre Dame, Indiana, next to South Bend; it was founded in 1842 by the Congregation of Holy Cross [1].

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind admission** for internationals: ability to pay is not considered. Need-based aid applicants must file the 2027–28 CSS Profile (code 1841) and documents through IDOC [3][4].
- **Aid deadlines are priority dates in the early rounds:** CSS Profile by Nov 15, 2026 for Restrictive Early Action and Jan 15, 2027 for Regular Decision [3][10].
- **Full-need and no-loan wording is on the general aid pages, not the international page.** The general pages say 100% of demonstrated need is met and offers contain only grants and scholarships [5][6]. The international page lists "student employment and University scholarships" among award types and does not mention a full-need promise [3]. Confirm with finaid@nd.edu that both apply to internationals.
- **Testing is optional for Fall 2027** (required from Fall 2028). Predicted scores can replace SAT/ACT for students with an IB Diploma, three or more CGE A-levels or the French Baccalaureate [8][9].
- **English test required** if your first language or schooling language is not English: TOEFL iBT, IELTS, PTE or Duolingo. Waived with SAT EBRW 650+ or ACT English or Reading 26+ [9].
- **No alumni or on-campus interviews;** an InitialView or Vericant interview is optional [9].
- **Restrictive Early Action:** you may not apply to any other school's binding ED I plan, and must withdraw if admitted to another school's ED II [11].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Admission is need-blind, so ability to pay is not considered [3][4]. The aid office's home page lists "100% of every student's demonstrated financial need met" under its stats [5]. The general costs page says offers are "composed of only grants and scholarships that do not need to be paid back"; families may still borrow on their own [6]. Neither page says it applies to international students, and the international page says awards "may include student employment and University scholarships" [3]. Our CSV records a full-need, no-loan policy announced Sept 13, 2024 for students entering in Fall 2025 or later, sourced to the international page; the announcement itself could not be fetched (HTTP 404 on the guessed URL).
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - 2027–28 CSS Profile (code 1841), open from Oct 1 [3].
  - Documents through IDOC: a parent U.S. or foreign tax return or non-filer statement, and 2025 parent earnings statements such as an official employer document showing annual salary [3].
  - Deadlines: Restrictive Early Action, CSS Profile Nov 15, 2026; Regular Decision, Jan 15, 2027 [3].
  - Certification of Finances: not mentioned [3].
- **What a package contains:** Grants and scholarships only per the general page [6]. Family contribution is estimated from income, assets (excluding retirement assets), private or parochial tuition, local cost of living, family size and the number of children in college [6]. Student contribution and summer earnings: not addressed.
- **Actual awards (CDS H6):** Not available, since the CDS PDFs return HTTP 403 (re-checked 2026-11-23).
- **Rules:**
  - Renewal: admission carries a four-year commitment. If family finances stay substantially the same, aid stays consistent; if they change, Notre Dame reviews and adjusts aid [6].
  - Outside scholarships: they reduce work-study or campus employment first, then university scholarship. Report them to the Office of Financial Aid, which may revise the offer [27].
  - Late aid applications and aid for transfers: Not published on the pages checked (nd.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Merit scholarships:** A limited number are offered to admitted first-years for accomplishment, leadership, service and intellectual promise; need is not a factor unless noted. Merit plus need-based aid may reduce need-based aid under federal rules and university policy. REA applicants get priority [3][11].
- **Net price calculator:** https://npc.collegeboard.org/app/nd [6]. The page does not say whether it works for internationals.

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common Application, Apply Coalition on Scoir, or the QuestBridge Application; QuestBridge applicants need not also file the Common App or Coalition [10][12]. Whether international students may use QuestBridge: Not stated.
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [10][11]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Aid priority (CSS Profile) | Decision |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Restrictive Early Action | Nov 1, 2026 | Nov 15, 2026 | Mid-December (before Christmas) |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 4, 2027 (the aid page says Jan 1, 2027) | Jan 15, 2027 | Mid- to late March |

  Reply date: May 1, with a $500 enrollment deposit [11]. The how-to-apply page does not label its year, so the dates are the ones shown for the current cycle; the aid page labels Nov 1, 2026 and Jan 2027 [3].
- **Fee and fee waivers:** $85, non-refundable. Waivable automatically through the Common App or Coalition Application, including for international applicants per the international page [9][10].
- **Required parts:** the application; one required Notre Dame Writing Supplement essay (150 words maximum) and two short answers of 50–100 words from four prompts; official transcripts; one core academic teacher evaluation; a counselor letter (strongly recommended, not required). The mid-year report is not required, though REA applicants may send a first-quarter summary [10].
- **English proficiency:** Required if your first language is not English or your schooling was not mainly in English. Accepted: TOEFL iBT, IELTS, PTE Academic, Duolingo English Test. Recommended minimums: TOEFL iBT 100 overall with Speaking 25 (old scale) or 5.5 average with no section below 5.0 (new scale); IELTS 7.5; Duolingo 125. Scores go directly from the testing agency (TOEFL code 1841). Waived with SAT EBRW 650+ or ACT English/Reading 26+; TOEFL Special Home Edition is not accepted [9].
- **International documents:** Official transcript with the Secondary School Report, with national exam results or predicted results (GCSE, A-level, IB). Cambridge curriculum applicants submit GCSE/IGCSE results and predicted A-levels, usually three or more A-level subjects. Translation rules: Not stated [9].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 98% (IPEDS Fall 2025 summary for the Fall 2024 cohort, rounded) [2]; College Scorecard gives 99.1% for full-time students [18].
- **Graduation (IPEDS via College Scorecard):** 89.8% within 4 years and 95.4% within 6 years; 94.4% within 6 years for nonresident students [18].
- **After graduation (Class of 2025, school figures):**
  - 97% placement within six months (jobs, graduate school or service) [20].
  - Plans: 63% full-time work, 20% graduate school, 4% service, 3% military, 7% other, 3% still seeking [20].
  - Self-reported median starting salary: $82,000 [20].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings 10 years after entry are $113,124 [18]. These cover federal-aid recipients only, so they exclude internationals.
- **Retention (school figure):** 99% from first to second year [26].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 8% of undergraduates are U.S. nonresidents, and 7% of first-time degree-seeking students come from foreign countries (IPEDS Fall 2025, rounded) [2]. Notre Dame says more than 1,800 international students from close to 100 countries attend, at all levels [26].
- **Support:** International Student and Scholar Affairs (ISSA), 105 Main Building, issa@nd.edu, helps with immigration responsibilities and links to OPT, CPT and arrival pages. Admitted students who enroll receive a Form I-20 for the F-1 visa; visa information is at issa.nd.edu [13][9]. Orientation: International Student Orientation is required for every new student on a Notre Dame F-1 or J-1 document. The Fall 2026 undergraduate session was Thursday, August 20; it's separate from Welcome Weekend, and both are required. Contact issa@nd.edu, 574-631-1138 [34].
- **Work:** F-1 students request CPT authorization and OPT through the ISSAlink portal [29]. Social Security numbers are for people authorized to work with evidence of employment [13].
- **Practicalities:**
  - Residence halls close for winter break (noon on December 20, 2026), so plan to travel or find other housing [30].
  - ISSA runs an International Ambassadors student leadership team [31].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** Undergraduates must live on campus for their first six semesters [32]. 82% of undergraduates live in 33 residence halls [26].
- **Location:** Notre Dame, Indiana, next to South Bend [1].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at S Bend, IN, 0.8 km from campus: average temperature 49.8°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 19.7°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 81.4°F; 40.87 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [22].
- **Safety:** The 2025 Security & Fire Safety Annual Report, with crime statistics for 2022–2024 for the main campus and Rome, is at https://police.nd.edu/assets/632444/2025_annual_safety_report.pdf [33].
- **Student body:** IPEDS gives 48% women among undergraduates (Fall 2025) [2].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:**
  - F-1 and J-1 students are enrolled automatically in Aetna Student Health. You can waive it only with proof of comparable coverage, and the waiver has to be filed every year by the deadline; otherwise you pay the premium [21].
  - 2026–27 annual premium: $3,046 [19].
  - Questions: insurancequestions@nd.edu [15].
- **Services:** University Health Services is by appointment, Monday to Friday 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., booked through the Patient Portal or 574-631-7497. A University Counseling Center exists [15].
