# Harvard University

Harvard is a private university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, founded in 1636 [19]. Its undergraduate school is Harvard College, which offers 50 concentrations (majors) [10].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind, full need, same aid as US students.** Admissions decisions are made without regard to aid, "Harvard meets each student's demonstrated need", and international students are eligible for the same aid as US students [2][4]. Aid is 100% need-based; there are no merit awards [4]. Aid applies from the first admit round on: REA and RD applicants are treated alike for full need [13].
- **Cheap for lower-income families.** Families earning under $100,000 with typical assets pay nothing toward billed costs (and aid also covers a $2,000 first-year start-up grant and travel); families up to $200,000 have tuition covered [4][20][21]. The pages do not say how these thresholds are applied to non-US incomes.
- **Forms.** Every aid applicant files the CSS Profile (code 3434) and uploads tax documents to IDOC. Non-US students who cannot pay the CSS fee, or whose country the College Board cannot take payment from, may use the Alternate Application with the Financial Statement for Students from Foreign Countries [5][6]. Aid deadlines: Nov 1 (REA), Feb 1 (RD) [5].
- **Testing is required for everyone.** SAT or ACT is required. AP, IB, GCSE/A-level or national leaving exam results are accepted only in exceptional cases when the SAT or ACT is not accessible. TOEFL, IELTS and Duolingo cannot replace them; English tests are not required [2][8].
- **Extremely selective.** 4.2% admitted in Fall 2025 (CDS C1) [1]. About 15% of the Class of 2030 is international, counted by permanent address (so it includes US citizens abroad) [7]. Harvard does not publish international applicant or admit counts in its CDS or on the pages read.
- **Early rule.** Restrictive Early Action bars applying early to any other private university; early applications to public universities or universities outside the US are allowed [13].
- **Aid actually given.** CDS 2025–26 H6: 805 nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid, averaging $83,013 (total $66,826,244) [1].

- **Test policy for Fall 2027:** SAT or ACT required (CDS C8: "required to be considered for admission"). In exceptional cases, when the tests are not accessible, AP, IB, GCSE/A-level or national leaving exam results (actual or predicted) can be used. Self-reported scores are accepted; official scores are required on enrollment. There are no score cutoffs [1][2][3]. Non-US citizens at non-US schools are typically not eligible for SAT/ACT fee waivers [2]. There is no separate rule only for internationals.
- **What the school weighs (CDS C7):** Harvard marks no factor "Very important" or "Important". It marks as "Considered": rigor of secondary school record, GPA, test scores, essay, recommendations, interview, extracurriculars, talent, character, first-generation status, alumni relation, geographic residence, volunteer work and work experience. Applicants may not apply to another private university's early program (ED, EA or REA), or a binding early program at a public university. Deferred applicants may apply ED II elsewhere. Harvard will meet full need whether you apply REA or RD [13]. No limit on international aid applicants in REA is stated.

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Admissions is need-blind. Aid is 100% need-based with no merit awards. International students are eligible for the same aid as US students; Harvard says it offers need-based aid "regardless of nationality or citizenship" [2][4][20].
- **Parent contribution:** Under $100,000 income (typical assets): no parent contribution and aid covers all billed expenses. Up to $200,000: aid covers at least tuition. Above $200,000: tailored aid. Significant assets count; home equity and retirement assets typically do not [4][20].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - CSS Profile, school code 3434, due Nov 1 (REA and QuestBridge Match), Feb 1 (RD), Mar 1 (transfer) [5].
  - IDOC upload of tax and income documents, opened only after the CSS Profile is complete. Non-English tax documents need an English translation, which the student may write on the document. Parents who file no tax return submit a non-filer statement or a wage statement, pay slip or employer letter [5].
  - Alternate Application for Financial Aid (non-US students only), for those who cannot afford or pay the CSS fee, or have unreliable internet: Financial Statement for Students from Foreign Countries plus 2025 tax documents, uploaded or mailed [6]. The CSS Profile and this statement are interchangeable and the CSS Profile is preferred [8].
  - REA and QuestBridge applicants get an aid estimate in mid-December; REA and RD applicants get a final decision around April 1 [5][6].
- **What a package contains:** Scholarship grants plus a student contribution through work (about $3,500 a year for personal expenses, "usually through work-study hours"), with a $2,000 start-up grant in the first year and a $2,000 launch grant in junior year. Students are never required to borrow [4][21]. All aid recipients, international and domestic, get a travel allowance in the award [2].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 805 degree-seeking nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid, average $83,013, total $66,826,244. Harvard's CDS marks institutional need-based aid as available to nonresidents [1]. The CDS does not state whether the count is need-based only. Against 1,021 nonresident undergraduates that is about 79% (our calculation; the CDS aid year and the enrollment year may differ).
- **Rules:**
  - Renewal: "You must apply for financial aid each year"; Harvard meets demonstrated need for all four years [8].
  - Late applications are accepted but may delay the decision; late tax filing does not affect eligibility [5].
  - Outside scholarships (report through OARS) first reduce the term-time work expectation, then replace an equal amount of Harvard scholarship; they cannot replace the parent contribution [8].
  - Transfers: aid deadline Mar 1 [5].
  - Applying for aid later: The pages read do not say whether a student who did not apply at admission can get aid later.
- **Merit scholarships:** None [4].
- **Net price calculator:** https://college.harvard.edu/financial-aid/net-price-calculator. International students can use it, but it uses average US living costs and taxes, so it may be less accurate for them [12].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App plus the Harvard supplement. SlideRoom for optional arts material. QuestBridge National College Match: "All students attending high school in the United States are eligible"; abroad, only US citizens and permanent residents are, so a non-US student at a non-US school appears not to be eligible [3][14].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027:** REA Nov 1, 2026 (decision mid-December); RD Jan 1, 2027 (decision end of March); reply by May 1; counselors may send materials up to a week later [9][13].
- **Fee:** $95, waived on request if it is a hardship and the student plans to apply for aid. Request it through the Common App (economic need indicators) or Harvard's FAQ process [8][9].
- **Required parts:** Common App essay; Harvard supplement of five short answers (150 words each: how your experiences let you contribute, a disagreement and what you learned, activities or family responsibilities that shaped you, how you will use a Harvard education, and three things roommates should know); Secondary School Report with transcript and counselor letter; two teacher evaluations from different subjects; Midyear School Report; Final report for admitted students by July 1. Optional scholarly, creative or arts materials [3][13].
- **English proficiency:** Not required. TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo scores may be submitted but cannot satisfy the testing requirement [2][8].
- **International documents:** Credentials from non-US schools may need specialized review. Translations are accepted, are not required to come from a professional translator (an English teacher is fine), and must show the translator's name and relationship to you, with a copy of the original attached [2]. Predicted exam results may be used only in the exceptional-case testing alternative [2].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** 50 concentrations, with secondary fields (minors) and special concentrations [10]. Largest groups in CDS 2025–26 J1 (bachelor's degrees by field): social sciences 27%, mathematics and statistics 14%, biological sciences 13%, computer and information sciences 11%, physical sciences 6%, psychology 5% [1]. Curriculum is concentration plus General Education and distribution requirements; the page excerpt did not give the Gen Ed detail [10].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 27%, biological and biomedical sciences 13%, mathematics and statistics 11%, computer and information sciences 10%, history 7% [35].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 11 to 1 for all students; the CDS gives a separate undergraduate ratio of 7 (CDS I2) [1]. Fall 2025 undergraduate class sections (I3): 482 with 2–9 students, 303 with 10–19, 84 with 20–29, 42 with 30–39, 28 with 40–49, 78 with 50–99, 49 with 100+ (1,066 in total) [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering and computer science are available: Applied Math, Biomedical Engineering, Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, Engineering Sciences, Environmental Science and Engineering, Mechanical Engineering [10]. Pre-health advising: Pre-med advising is organized through the residential Houses, each with its own pre-med advisers and timelines (for example, Dudley House asks for a survey by April 1 and 4–6 letters by May 1 of the application year) [37]. Pre-law advising and 3-2 or dual-degree programs: Not published on the pages checked (harvard.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Opportunities:**
  - Research is built into some concentrations, for example the Chemistry and Applied Math theses [10].
  - Study abroad: sophomores, juniors and seniors can spend a semester abroad. The Office of International Education wants credit paperwork by October 1 (spring) or March 1 (fall) [27]. Participation rates weren't published on the pages checked.
  - Cross-registration: since Fall 2024, Harvard College students can take unlimited cross-registered courses (including at MIT). They count toward the 128-credit requirement but not the GPA, unless failed. First-years need a statement of interest to their Resident Dean before taking MIT courses [25].
- **STEM majors:** The STEM OPT extension applies to STEM-designated degrees; Harvard publishes no list of designated concentrations. Compare the CIP code on your I-20 with the DHS STEM list, and ask your HIO adviser if you're unsure [15][34].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 98.0% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,608 of 1,641 Fall 2024 entrants returned) [1]. Fall 2019 cohort: 61.5% graduated within 4 years (1,011 of 1,644) and 97.1% within 6 years (1,596 of 1,644) (CDS B4–B11, our calculation) [1]. The Fall 2018 cohort 6-year rate was 97.6% [1].
- **After graduation:** The most recent senior-survey "next steps" graphics Harvard posts cover only classes 2011–2019. Harvard says historically about 18% of each class goes to medical school and into health careers [18]. A current first-destination report and top employers: Not published on the pages checked (harvard.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard earnings were not retrievable (the API was rate-limited and the Scorecard page returned HTTP 403). These figures cover only federal-aid recipients and exclude international students.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 1,021 degree-seeking nonresident undergraduates (15.3%) and 251 nonresident first-years (15.0% of 1,673) (CDS B2) [1]. About 15% of the Class of 2030 is international by address [7]. Number of countries: Not published on the pages read.
- **Support:** The Harvard International Office (HIO) handles visas and immigration, I-20s and work authorization. HIO's own pages returned HTTP 403, so details below come from search-engine index text [15]. Admitted international students are told how to apply for the student visa [8].
- **Work:** F-1 students may work on campus up to 20 hours a week in term (full-time in breaks), starting no earlier than 30 days before classes; no off-campus work in the first year. CPT is available before graduation. OPT gives 12 months per degree level, after two consecutive semesters in status; STEM degrees may extend by 24 months with an I-983 plan and an E-Verify employer [15].
- **Break housing:** You must apply for Winter Recess housing to stay on campus over the break. For Fall 2026, Round I closed October 19 and Round II (with a $50 fee) closed November 16. You need to have lived on campus in the fall and spring terms; students on leave or studying abroad are generally not approved [28].
- **Mentor programs and student organizations:** Not published on the pages checked (harvard.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** "Harvard guarantees housing for all four years." First-years live in one of 17 dorms in or near Harvard Yard; students are assigned to one of 12 Houses for the last three years [11]. CDS 2025–26 F1: 100% of first-years and 97% of undergraduates live in college-owned or affiliated housing; 86% of first-years and 85% of undergraduates are from out of state (international students excluded) [1].
- **Location:** Cambridge, next to Boston. Transport: from Boston Logan, take the Silver Line SL1 to South Station, then the Red Line toward Alewife to Harvard, about 45–55 minutes. A taxi takes 20–25 minutes and costs $35–40 plus tip [36].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Jamaica Plain, MA, 8 km from campus: average temperature 51.2°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 22.0°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 82.2°F; 49.36 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [33].
- **Safety:** The Harvard University Police Department publishes the 2026 Annual Security Report (Clery Act), covering calendar years 2023–2025: https://www.hupd.harvard.edu/annual-security-report [17]. The statistics pages returned HTTP 403; no figures were read.
- **Student body:** CDS B1: among 6,738 undergraduates, 3,117 men and 3,621 women [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** Harvard College lists health insurance at $4,954 for 2026–27, required unless you are covered under a family plan [4]. International waiver rules: see the note under section 5 [15].
- **Services:**
  - Harvard University Health Services (HUHS): a multi-specialty medical practice just for the Harvard community, 617-495-5711 [29][30].
  - Counseling and Mental Health Services (CAMHS): free and confidential for all enrolled students, with a 24/7 line, CAMHS Cares (617-495-2042) [29].
  - Disability Access Office (DAO): register and submit documentation to request accommodations. The DAO lists its address as 1414 Massachusetts Avenue, 3R, Cambridge; older pages list the Smith Campus Center [31].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Age:** Harvard's charter dates from 1650, and it calls itself the oldest US institution of higher education [19].
- **US presidents:** Harvard's Gazette counted eight presidents with Harvard degrees when Barack Obama took office in 2009: John Adams, John Quincy Adams, Rutherford B. Hayes, Theodore Roosevelt, Franklin D. Roosevelt, John F. Kennedy, George W. Bush (MBA) and Obama [22].
- **Alumni abroad:** the Harvard Club of Spain lists Benazir Bhutto (1973), Prime Minister of Pakistan, and Ellen Johnson Sirleaf (1971), President of Liberia [23].
- **Nobel laureates:** Harvard's history timeline calls Theodore William Richards (Chemistry) "the first of 50 Harvard Nobel laureates". Counts depend on how affiliation is defined [24].
