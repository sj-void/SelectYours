# Bowdoin College

Bowdoin is a private liberal arts college in Brunswick, Maine, with about 1,840 undergraduates and no graduate students [2][1].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **One of the few need-blind colleges for internationals.** Bowdoin is need-blind for international students and meets their full calculated need with scholarships rather than loans [3].
- **But the odds are very long.** In Fall 2025, 76 of 6,041 international applicants were admitted (about 1.3%), against 6.8% overall. 37 internationals enrolled (CDS 2025–26 C1) [2].
- **Real aid numbers.** 102 international undergraduates received Bowdoin aid, averaging $84,882, or $8.66 million in total (CDS 2025–26 H6) [2].
- **CSS Profile required; ISFAA not accepted** [3]. International applicants (except Canadians) submit aid materials only if admitted [7].
- **No aid for international transfers, as a rule.** Bowdoin "does not typically" have need-based aid for international transfer students [3].
- **No English-test minimum, but no ESL courses.** English tests are optional, with no minimum score. Bowdoin offers no English as a Second Language courses, so students must already be fluent at college level [6].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded:** Chartered in 1794 and opened 2 September 1802. It is named for Governor James Bowdoin II [8].
- **Campus:** a 207-acre main campus around a central quad in Brunswick, Maine [9]. Bowdoin has also acquired 130 acres at the former Brunswick Naval Air Station [9]. The Schiller Coastal Studies Center is a 118-acre site on Orr's Island with marine and land labs [10].
- **Enrollment:** 1,841 students, all undergraduates (CDS 2025–26 B1) [2].
- **Housing:** 100% of first-years and 94.5% of all undergraduates live in college housing (CDS 2025–26 F1) [2].
- **Nearest airport:** Portland International Jetport, about 30 minutes' drive, with direct flights to about 15 major cities [11].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-blind for internationals. Meets full calculated need with scholarships, not loans [3].
- **Forms:** CSS Profile, required; Bowdoin does not accept the ISFAA [3]. Aid documents are submitted only after admission, except by Canadians [7].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 102 aided international undergraduates; average $84,882; total $8,657,920 [2].
- **Transfers:** Need-based aid is not typically available to international transfer students [3].
- Renewal and outside-scholarship rules: Not published on the pages checked (bowdoin.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** more than 40 majors [10].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 34%, biological and biomedical sciences 21%, physical sciences 9%, computer and information sciences 8%, area, ethnic and gender studies 5% [16].
- **Teaching:** 8:1 student–faculty ratio; 71% of classes have fewer than 20 students [10]. The CDS 2025–26 gives the same 8 to 1 ratio (I2) [2].
- **Research:** the Schiller Coastal Studies Center supports marine and land research [10].
- **STEM-designated majors:** Not published on the pages checked (bowdoin.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 96.8%; 6-year graduation rate 92.8% (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [1].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard has earnings data [1]. It covers federal-aid recipients only and excludes international students.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 119 nonresident degree-seeking undergraduates (CDS 2025–26 B2) [2].
- **Support:** The Associate Class Dean is Bowdoin's Designated School Official. She handles OPT and CPT applications, Social Security numbers and cultural adjustment [12].
- **Orientation:** International Student Orientation is a required three-day program for F-1 and J-1 students. For 2026, students had to arrive by 21 August, before 5 p.m. [13].
- **Work:**
  - OPT: up to 12 months, before or after completing studies. Pre-completion OPT needs one full academic year of enrollment [12].
  - CPT: must relate directly to your major, happens only in summer, and requires a job offer before you apply. Unpaid internships need CPT too [12].
- **Break housing:** Not published on the pages checked (bowdoin.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Break housing:** Students whose home is outside the US can be approved to stay over winter break; apply through the housing portal after Thanksgiving break [18].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** 100% of first-years and 94.5% of undergraduates live in college housing (CDS F1) [2].
- **Location:** Brunswick, Maine, about 30 minutes from Portland's airport [11].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Brunswick Nas, ME, 2.6 km from campus: average temperature 45.7°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 15.1°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 76.7°F; 50.01 inches of precipitation a year, including 66.8 inches of snowfall [14].
- **Annual Security Report (Clery Act):** Bowdoin's report "2023, 2024, and 2025 Campus Crime, Fire, Alcohol, and Illegal Drugs" (labelled for 2026–27) is at https://www.bowdoin.edu/security/pdf/1-asfsr-2023-24-25.pdf [15].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The 2026–27 health plan costs $4,442. You can waive it online with adequate coverage, and aided students who need the plan can ask for its cost to be covered by extra aid [20].
- **Counseling and disability services:** The Student Accessibility Office reviews documentation and issues a written list of accommodations; students ask it to notify faculty each semester, and temporary accommodations are possible while documentation is gathered. Health Services and the Counseling Center can refer students for testing [17].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Strengths:**
  - need-blind admission for internationals with no-loan aid [3];
  - the Schiller Coastal Studies Center [10];
  - small classes: 71% have fewer than 20 students [10].
- **Notable alumni:** Bowdoin's list includes Reed Hastings (1983), founder of Netflix; Franklin Pierce (1824), 14th US president; and the poet Henry Wadsworth Longfellow (1825) [19].
