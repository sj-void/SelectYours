# Stanford University

Stanford is a private nonprofit research university at Stanford, California, founded in 1885 and opened in 1891, on an 8,353-acre campus [9][12].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-aware, with full need for those admitted.** Stanford says an international applicant's request for aid "will be a factor in our admission evaluation," and also that "we will meet the full need of all admitted students regardless of citizenship" [2].
- **Ask for aid on the application or lose the chance.** International students who do not request aid at admission cannot apply for it during their four years, except if citizenship status changes and they become eligible for federal aid [2][7].
- **Early rounds are open to aid applicants.** Restrictive Early Action international applicants file the CSS Profile (code 4704) and the International Student Supplement by Nov 15, with aid notification in mid-December; Regular Decision applicants file by Feb 15 with notification in April [8][14].
- **Aid for transfers exists.** Stanford's financial aid office lists a checklist for international transfer applicants (priority date Mar 15, notification in May) [15]. The admissions page does not describe transfer aid [2].
- **Actual awards.** In CDS 2025–26 H6, 260 nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid, averaging $85,424 (total $22,210,145) [1].
- **No merit scholarships** except a limited number of athletic scholarships [2].
- **SAT or ACT required.** No English proficiency test is required; scores may be self-reported voluntarily, and fluency in English is a prerequisite. An English test cannot replace the SAT/ACT [2][5].
- **Very selective.** 2,302 of 60,646 applicants were admitted for Fall 2025 (CDS C1) [1]. International applicant counts are not published in the CDS (residency breakdown blank) [1].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded; campus:** Founded in 1885 and opened in 1891; 8,353 acres in total [12].
- **Calendar:** Quarter system (enrollment is reported by quarter, for example Autumn Quarter 2025) [12]. Enrollment is for fall only [2].
- **Enrollment:** Stanford Facts gives 7,289 undergraduates and 10,025 graduate students in Autumn Quarter 2025 [12]. CDS 2025–26 B1 gives 7,346 total undergraduates (7,289 degree-seeking), 10,775 graduate students and 18,121 in all [1].
- **Schools and colleges:** Not published on the pages checked (stanford.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Nearest airport:** San Jose (SJC) is 20 miles south and San Francisco (SFO) about 25 miles north. Caltrain serves Palo Alto station, and the free Marguerite shuttle runs to campus [30].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** U.S. citizens, permanent residents, undocumented/DACA students and eligible noncitizens are need-blind. International applicants who request aid are evaluated need-aware because "Stanford has a limited amount of financial aid for international students." Stanford will meet the full need of all admitted students regardless of citizenship [2][7]. The financial aid office says "for all but some international applicants, financial status will not affect the admission decision" [16].
- **Forms and deadlines (Fall 2027 entry, aid year 2027–28):**
  - Indicate on the admission application that you are applying for aid [8][14].
  - CSS Profile, school code 4704. Divorced, separated or never-married parents each file their own information [8][14].
  - International Student Supplement (signed), uploaded through the Financial Aid Office's Document Upload [14].
  - The paper International Student Application for Financial Assistance only if you cannot pay the CSS fee or your country cannot process College Board payments [8][14].
  - Canadian citizens: IDOC with signed 2025 Canadian tax statements, and no International Student Supplement [8][14].
  - Dates: CSS Profile opens Oct 1. REA: priority Nov 15, notification mid-December. RD: priority Feb 15, notification April [8][14].
- **What a package contains:** Stanford says aid is scholarships that need not be repaid and it does "not expect you to take out student loans" [10]. The pages read do not give a student contribution or summer-earnings figure for internationals [10]. Awards need a Social Security number or ITIN to be paid out (not needed to apply) [7].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6, nonresident undergraduates):** 260 aided (need-based or non-need-based institutional aid); average $85,424; total $22,210,145 [1].
- **Rules:**
  - Late requests: not allowed, except after a citizenship change that makes you federal-aid eligible [2][7].
  - Transfers: the aid office lists an international transfer checklist (priority Mar 15, notification May); the admissions page says nothing on transfer aid [15][2].
  - Outside scholarships are listed as an additional funding source; how they reduce awards is not stated [10].
  - Renewal rules: Not published on the pages checked (stanford.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Merit scholarships:** None, apart from a limited number of athletic scholarships [2].
- **Tuition-free thresholds (all undergraduates; not said to apply to internationals):** families under $150,000 with typical assets pay no tuition; under $100,000 pay no tuition, room or board [10].
- **Net price calculator:** https://financialaid.stanford.edu/undergrad/how/calculator/index.html?tab=quick-cost-estimator [10]. Whether it works for internationals: not stated.

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common Application only on the page read [4]. QuestBridge's National College Match is open only to students at US high schools (any citizenship) and to US citizens and permanent residents abroad [24].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 (the pages give no year) [4]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Decision | Reply by |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Restrictive Early Action | Nov 1 (Oct 15 if submitting an arts portfolio; portfolio materials by Oct 20) | Mid-December | May 1 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 5 (Dec 5 with an arts portfolio; materials by Dec 10) | Early April | May 1 |

- **Fee and waivers:** $100, nonrefundable, or a fee waiver request [4].
- **Required parts:** Common App including essays; SAT or ACT; School Report and counselor letter; official transcripts; two academic teacher recommendations; midyear transcript due Feb 15. Applicants are limited to three applications to Stanford in total (first-year and transfer combined) [4]. Essay prompts: Not published on the pages checked (the essays page wasn't reachable).
- **English proficiency:** No English test is required; applicants may self-report scores. No minimum scores are listed; English fluency is a prerequisite [2].
- **International documents:** Translate transcripts and recommendations into English, with originals; a school official should translate. Marks or predicted grades must come directly from the school. No GPA conversion needed; no curriculum preferred. IB applicants outside the U.S.: school sends predicted marks. British system: GCSE results and predicted A-Levels. Without a counselor, another school official may submit the counselor recommendation [2][5]. Stanford cannot process applications from people in or ordinarily living in Iran, due to U.S. Treasury sanctions (as of Aug 24, 2026) [2].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** Number of majors and minors and curriculum style: Not published on the pages checked (stanford.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** computer and information sciences 21%, interdisciplinary studies 19%, social sciences 15%, engineering 15%, mathematics and statistics 6% [28].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 10 to 1 (CDS 2025–26 I2: 14,211 students, 1,359 faculty). Class sections with undergraduates (CDS I3): 599 of 2–9 students, 627 of 10–19, 168 of 20–29, 80 of 30–39, 61 of 40–49, 144 of 50–99, 80 of 100+ (1,759 in total) [1].
- **Fields:** Pre-health advising: Stanford's academic advisers work with pre-med and pre-law students; Stanford has no committee letter, so medical-school applicants send individual letters [34]. Engineering, computer science, pre-law and dual-degree programs: Not published on the pages checked (stanford.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Opportunities:** There are no undergraduate exchange programs for international applicants [2]. Study abroad and research: About 50% of each graduating class takes part in a Bing Overseas Studies Program, and more than 1,000 undergraduates do in-depth research each year. Honors details weren't published on the pages checked [33].
- **STEM majors:** Bechtel International Center publishes no list. Your degree code on the I-20 must be on the federal STEM Designated Degree Program List. STEM OPT adds 24 months to the 12-month post-completion OPT. It needs an E-Verify employer, a job related to the field, and at least 20 hours a week; apply up to 90 days before your OPT ends [25].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 97.46% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,650 of 1,693) [1]. Six-year graduation rate 91.64% for the Fall 2019 cohort (1,556 of 1,698). Of that cohort 770 graduated within four years (45.4%, our calculation) and 1,403 within five years (82.6%, our calculation; 770 + 633) [1]. College Scorecard shows 45.35% within four years and 91.64% within six [13]; the low four-year rate is as published and the sources do not explain it.
- **After graduation:** No university-wide first-destination report for undergraduates on the pages checked (stanford.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings $125,340 ten years after entry and $112,810 six years after entry. These cover federal-aid recipients only and exclude international students [13].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 965 nonresident undergraduates (CDS B2), of whom 237 are first-years [1]. The Class of 2030 represents 72 countries [23].
- **Support:** The Bechtel International Center: initial I-20/DS-2019 requests are made in Bechtel Connect after accepting admission; undergraduate orientation covers visas, immigration documents and legal status; new students may see an enrollment hold. It runs up to 100 events a year. Contact: internationalstudents@stanford.edu [17].
- **Work (F-1) [17]:**
  - On-campus work: eligibility may begin in the first quarter.
  - CPT: needs authorization before starting. Bechtel reports a federal court injunction against August 2026 federal guidance that had narrowed CPT eligibility; check current status.
  - OPT: pre-completion, post-completion and a 24-month STEM extension for eligible STEM graduates.
- **Practicalities:**
  - Winter closure: Student Affairs publishes a winter-closure resource list each year. In 2025–26, CAPS had on-call clinicians at Vaden, and Public Safety was reachable 24/7 at 650.329.2413 [26].
  - Bechtel lists coffee socials and Bay Area trips [17].
  - Mentor programs: Bechtel International Center's SIB Mentors program pairs incoming international undergraduates with two trained upper-class mentors, with each mentor pair supporting about ten first-years [32]. Break housing: Undergraduate residences close for winter break; students return at winter-quarter move-in [29].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** CDS F1: 100% of first-year students and 93% of undergraduates live in college-owned housing [1]. Housing guarantee: Stanford guarantees housing for undergraduates for four years (twelve quarters for first-years; transfer students get three years). All first-years live on campus, and about 98% of eligible undergraduates live in campus housing [35].
- **Location:** A large-suburb campus at Stanford, California (IPEDS locale 21) [13]. Transport: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Palo Alto, CA, 2.9 km from campus: average temperature 58.5°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 39.7°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 78.8°F; 15.12 inches of precipitation a year, including 0.0 inches of snowfall [22].
- **Safety:** The Stanford Department of Public Safety links its Annual Safety, Security and Fire Report at https://police.stanford.edu/safety-report.html; the current report is linked from that page [18].
- **Student body:** College Scorecard shows 48.4% men and 51.6% women among undergraduates [13]. CDS C1 applicants: 31,609 men and 29,011 women; enrolled 945 men and 894 women [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** Stanford requires all registered students to carry adequate health insurance, through Cardinal Care or a plan meeting its minimum coverage rules. The 2026–27 premium and the waiver rules for international students are not given on the page read; contact the Health Insurance Programs Office (650.723.2135) [19]. Stanford's budget assumes international students take Cardinal Care [3].
- **Services:**
  - Vaden Health Services is the student health center. In winter 2026 it closes December 24–27 [27].
  - Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS) is free to Stanford students: 650.723.3785, with 24/7 phone support when closed [27].
  - Disability services: the Office of Accessible Education works with students with disabilities; a Disability Adviser reviews each request. Accommodations linked to mental health need a letter from CAPS, which means being in treatment [31].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Founding:** Leland and Jane Lathrop Stanford founded the university in 1885 as a memorial to their only child [20].
- **Founders:** Stanford's history credits Larry Page and Sergey Brin, who developed PageRank as graduate students and founded Google in 1998. It also names Bill Hewlett and David Packard (Hewlett-Packard) and Scott McNealy (Sun Microsystems) [20].
- **Nobel laureates:** Stanford News puts the total at about three dozen, with about 20 to 22 living [21]. Counts depend on how affiliation is defined.
