# Colorado College

Colorado College is a private liberal arts college in Colorado Springs, Colorado, with about 2,000 undergraduates. It teaches on the Block Plan: one course at a time, in 3.5-week blocks [1][2].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

No Common Data Set was read in this pass, so admissions numbers come from federal IPEDS data (Fall 2024) [1].

## Key points for international applicants

- **The most competitive group.** Colorado College calls international aid applicants its most competitive group: it can "fully fund only a handful of students out of thousands of applicants" [4].
- **Decide at application.** Request aid when you apply. If you mark "no", you can't change your mind for two years. Students who don't apply or don't qualify for need-based aid at admission generally get no institutional aid later [4].
- **CSS Profile or CC ISFAA.** Non-UWC international applicants no longer get CSS fee waivers; they may file the CSS Profile (and pay its fees) or the free Colorado College ISFAA. UWC applicants get fee waivers [4].
- **Aid counts travel home.** The cost of attendance used for aid includes tuition, housing, meals, health insurance, books, personal expenses and travel to and from your home country [4].
- **Non-aid applicants must prove full funding** for eight semesters [4].
- **Work-study up to $2,100** may be part of the package (our list) [3].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Full funding for "a handful" of international applicants each year [4]. Our list's full-need classification rests on a general pledge to all admitted students and is rated medium confidence [3].
- **Forms:** CSS Profile (fees payable unless UWC) or the CC ISFAA [4]. The CSS Profile opens after Oct 1; documents are due by your admission deadline [5].
- **Rules:** Aid must be requested at admission [4].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–2026 H6):** average award $52,713 and total $5,113,171 to nonresident undergraduates [25]. The printed count (73) doesn't match total ÷ average (about 97), so check the PDF before relying on the count.

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App, QuestBridge, or Coalition with a writing supplement; no application fee [9].
- **Aid:** CSS Profile (fees apply unless UWC) or the free CC ISFAA, due with the admission application [4][5].
- **Testing and English proficiency:** Colorado College is test-optional for the SAT and ACT. English tests are required when English isn't your native language or your school's main language; the highest score counts, and a waiver can be requested if the exams are unavailable [21].

## 7. Academics

- **Block Plan:** one 3.5-week course at a time, eight blocks a year [6].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 19%, biological and biomedical sciences 17%, visual and performing arts 9%, business and marketing 8%, natural resources 7% [17].
- **STEM OPT:** eligible STEM majors can work up to 36 months in total after graduation [10].
- **Majors:** 42 majors and 33 minors (another college page says 80 majors and minors) [24].
- **Class sizes (CDS 2025–26 I3):** 387 of 514 class sections (75%) had fewer than 20 students [25]. The PDF text scrambles the columns, so they were matched by checking that the seven size bands add up to the total.

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 93.6%; 6-year graduation rate 86.2% (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [1].

## 9. International student life

- **Office:** International Student and Scholar Services (ISSS) [11].
- **Orientation:** International Student Orientation comes before New Student Orientation. The Fall 2026 arrival date was 12 August [11].
- **I-20 and entry:** The I-20 is issued after admission. You may enter the US up to 30 days before the program start date [12].
- **OPT:** Request an OPT I-20, attend a workshop and file with USCIS within 30 days of the I-20 issue date. Jobs must be at least 20 hours a week and related to your major [13].
- **Travel:** I-20 travel signatures last one year, or six months while on OPT [14].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Setting:** Colorado Springs, Colorado [1].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Colorado Springs Municipal Airport, CO, 12.4 km from campus: average temperature 50.4°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 19.1°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 83.9°F; 15.91 inches of precipitation a year, including 32.5 inches of snowfall [15].
- **Housing:** confirming on-campus housing is on ISSS's pre-arrival checklist [12].
- **Annual Security Report:** The 2026 Annual Security & Fire Safety Report is at https://www.coloradocollege.edu/offices/campussafety/campus-crime-statistics/2026-Annual-Security-and-Fire-Safety-Report-Final.pdf [16].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The 2026–27 plan costs $4,975 a year ($2,081 fall, $2,894 spring) [22]. Health insurance is part of the cost the aid office uses to work out international need [4].
- **Counseling and disability services:** The Counseling Center is open to all enrolled students, with the first eight sessions free. Accessibility Resources reviews documentation and decides accommodations (for example extended exam time, a reduced-distraction setting, accessible texts); it also serves mental-health and temporary conditions [18].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Strengths:** the Block Plan [6]; a full-need pledge to every admitted student [9]; no application fee [9].
- **Notable alumni:** Alumni include Ken Salazar '77 (US Secretary of the Interior), Jane Lubchenco '69 (head of NOAA), Lori Garver '83 (NASA deputy administrator) and voice actor Dee Bradley Baker '86 [20].
