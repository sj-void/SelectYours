# Johns Hopkins University

Johns Hopkins is a private nonprofit research university in Baltimore, Maryland. Its undergraduate schools are the Krieger School of Arts & Sciences, the Whiting School of Engineering and the Peabody Institute [8][15].

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-aware, but full need with no loans.** Hopkins is need-blind for domestic applicants and need-aware for internationals who apply for aid. It promises to meet 100% of demonstrated need for admitted internationals "without any loans" [1][5].
- **Apply for aid now or never.** Internationals who might need aid at any point in four years must apply when they apply for admission. They cannot apply after decisions are released or in a later year [1][4][5]. International transfer students get no Hopkins aid [1][5].
- **Every international files a Certification of Finances (COF),** whether or not they ask for aid. Without it the application is incomplete. Aid applicants also file the CSS Profile (code 5332) or the free JHU International Student Financial Aid Application (ISFAA) [1][4].
- **About 10% of international students receive financial aid** [1][5].
- **The school is very selective for internationals.** In Fall 2025, 404 of 10,705 international applicants were admitted (3.8%) and 183 enrolled (CDS 2025–26 C1) [14]. The international share of the entering class fell from 13.5% in Fall 2025 to 9.7% in Fall 2026 [9].
- **SAT or ACT is required.** An English test is recommended if English is not your first language. It is encouraged if your SAT ERW is below 690, or if both your ACT Reading and English are below 30 [3].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-aware for internationals who apply for aid. Hopkins promises to meet "100% of demonstrated need for all admitted students, including international students" with no loans [1][5]. Aid offers never include loans; families may borrow on their own [5].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - Certification of Finances (COF), required of every international applicant. It is generated in the applicant portal after you apply and is due by the aid deadline [1][4].
  - CSS Profile (code 5332), or the free JHU ISFAA if you cannot complete the CSS Profile. The ISFAA takes 5 to 7 days to process, and the CSS Profile then shows as "Waived". Hopkins does not give CSS fee waivers to internationals [1][4][5].
  - Tax or income documents are not needed at first. Hopkins may ask for them later through IDOC [1][4].
  - Aid deadlines: ED I Nov 15, 2026; ED II Jan 15, 2027; RD Jan 15, 2027 [4].
- **What a package contains:** Need-based scholarships plus work opportunities, with no loans. Most students work 8–10 hours a week. The student contribution from summer savings is $1,800 in the first year and $2,600 in each later year [5].
- **Actual awards (CDS H6):** Not available. The CDS PDF sits behind a bot-protection challenge, and a search-engine extract showed unlabelled dollar figures that we don't use [14]. The average need-based scholarship for first-years is $70K (all students, school Fast Facts) [28]. The school says about 10% of international students receive aid [1][5].
- **Rules:**
  - Renewal: aid for internationals who get it in the first year renews automatically [5].
  - Late applicants: internationals who don't receive aid as first-years cannot get it later [1][5].
  - Outside scholarships first reduce the summer savings and work-study expectation. Anything beyond that reduces the Hopkins scholarship dollar for dollar [5].
  - Transfers: international transfer students are not eligible for Hopkins aid [1][5].
- **Merit scholarships:** A small number each year. All applicants are considered automatically, whatever their citizenship; there is no separate application [1][5].
- **Tuition Promise (from 2026–27):** Families earning up to $200,000 pay no tuition; families earning up to $100,000 pay $0 in total [5]. The page does not say whether this applies to internationals. International aid is described separately as "limited" [5].
- **Net price calculator:** College Board NPC, https://npc.collegeboard.org/app/jhu, and the MyinTuition quick estimator, https://c2calc.myintuitionapp.org/institution/3295dc7d-f579-40ac-8bd4-3bc96c995741/costestimator [6]. The page does not say whether either works for internationals [6].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App or Coalition on Scoir. There is no separate international application [1][2]. QuestBridge: Hopkins takes part in the QuestBridge National College Match [18], but QuestBridge limits the Match to seniors at US high schools and to US citizens and permanent residents living abroad, so internationals outside the US are not eligible [19].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [2]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Aid by | Decision | Reply by |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Early Decision I | Nov 1, 2026 | Nov 15, 2026 | Dec 11, 2026 | Jan 15, 2027 |
  | Early Decision II | Jan 2, 2027 | Jan 15, 2027 | Feb 12, 2027 | Feb 26, 2027 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 2, 2027 | Jan 15, 2027 | Mar 24, 2027 | May 1, 2027 |

- **Fee and fee waivers:** $70. Waivers can be requested through the Common App or Coalition, or through Hopkins' own fee waiver form. The page does not say whether internationals qualify [2].
- **Required parts:** the application with the personal statement; one Hopkins supplemental essay (350 words, on engaging across differences); test scores; the school report with counselor recommendation, transcript and school profile; two teacher evaluations from different academic subjects; the mid-year report (mid-February); and the ED agreement for ED applicants. Extra material (an extra recommendation, a portfolio, a resume) can be uploaded in the portal. Biomedical engineering applicants list BME as their first-choice major; there is no separate application [2].
- **English proficiency:** TOEFL, IELTS, Duolingo or Cambridge are recommended, not required. They are recommended if English is not your first language or you haven't been at an English-medium school for the last three years [1][3]. Typical competitive scores [3]:
  - TOEFL iBT: 5 overall on the new 1–6 scale (preferred sub-scores Reading 5.5, Listening 5.5, Writing 5, Speaking 5)
  - IELTS: 7.0 or higher in each band
  - Duolingo: 120 or higher
  - Cambridge: C1 Advanced or C2 Proficiency, scored 185 or higher
- **International documents:** Official records for the equivalents of grades 9–11, plus certificates or exam results showing completion of secondary school (for example O-levels, A-levels or IB). Non-English documents need a verified English translation. If your school doesn't use GPA or rank, leave them blank [1].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** "100+" undergraduate majors and minors [8]. The undergraduate studies page lists 51 majors and 42 minors at Homewood, with no core curriculum (open curriculum) [20]. Of Class of 2030 regular-decision admits, 62% planned to enrol in the Krieger School of Arts and Sciences and the rest in the Whiting School of Engineering [29].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** biological and biomedical sciences 23%, engineering 20%, social sciences 10%, computer and information sciences 10%, health professions 10% [31].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 9:1 and average class size 19 (school figures) [8]. College Scorecard (IPEDS) gives 6:1 [15]. CDS I2/I3: not available (CDS behind a bot-protection challenge) [14].
- **Fields:** Engineering is offered through the Whiting School [8]. Optional direct-matriculation programs lead to a master's in International Health or International Relations, or an MBA [2]. Pre-health and pre-law: 85% medical-school and 97% law-school admit rates for Hopkins applicants (school figures) [8]. Advising details: The Office of Pre-Professional Programs and Advising writes a committee letter (an evaluation, not a recommendation). It requires at least one individual advising appointment and letters including two academic ones; alumni can use the process for four years after graduating [33].
- **Opportunities:** "350+" approved study-abroad programs; 95% of students have at least one internship or research experience [8]. Cross-registration and honors: Not published on the pages checked.
- **STEM majors:** Undergraduates may be eligible for the 24-month STEM OPT extension [16]. OIS publishes no list of STEM-designated majors on the pages checked. Eligibility depends on the CIP code on your I-20 matching the federal STEM list; check with OIS [16].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 97.8% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,350 of the 1,380 Fall 2024 entrants returned) [14]; the school gives 97% [8]. Graduation rates (IPEDS via College Scorecard): 85.4% within 4 years and 93.4% within 6 years; 93.7% within 6 years for nonresident students [15]. The school gives 94% within six years [8]. CDS B4–B11: not available (CDS behind a bot-protection challenge) [14].
- **After graduation:** 98% employed full-time or in graduate school within six months; 36% go on to graduate or professional school (school figures, year not stated) [8]. The Life Design Lab publishes first-destination reports [21]; its figures weren't in a readable form, so top employers and graduate-school placements aren't given here.
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings are $104,590 ten years after entry and $86,265 six years after entry. These figures cover only federal-aid recipients and leave out international students [15].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** International students are 15.2% of degree-seeking undergraduates (College Scorecard) [15]; 9.7% of the Class of 2030 [8][9]. The undergraduate body comes from "80+" countries [8].
- **Support:** The Office of International Services (OIS). New F-1 and J-1 students complete check-in and the OIS orientation entirely online after arrival, starting in iHopkins. They may arrive up to 30 days before the program start date [16]. I-20: new students request an initial I-20 through iHopkins after accepting the offer and paying deposits; OIS issues it within 10 business days of a complete request [22].
- **Work:**
  - On campus: F-1 students may work up to 20 hours a week in term without OIS approval, and full-time in summer [1][5][16].
  - CPT: available for required practical training. 365 days or more of full-time CPT removes OPT eligibility [16].
  - Pre-completion OPT: only after one academic year in status [16].
  - Post-completion OPT: up to 12 months, with a 24-month STEM extension "available to some students" [16].
  - Hopkins lists "500+" on-campus work opportunities [8].
- **Practicalities:** All first- and second-year students must live in university housing [22][23]. OIS says on-campus housing is on the Homewood and Peabody campuses, and summer housing may be available for in-person summer classes [30]. Break housing, mentor programs and student organizations: Not published on the pages checked.

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** 93% of first-year students live on campus, with five residence-hall options for first-years (school figures) [8]. Hopkins requires all full-time Homewood undergraduates to live on campus for their first two years, with exemptions such as living with a parent or guardian nearby; housing for juniors and seniors is limited [23]. No guarantee of first-choice placement is given [18]. CDS F1: not available (CDS behind a bot-protection challenge).
- **Location:** Baltimore, a large city (IPEDS locale 11) [15]. Nearest airport: The closest major airport is BWI. The MARC train links BWI with Penn Station, where a free JHU shuttle serves Homewood [32].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Md Sci Ctr Baltimore, MD, 5.4 km from campus: average temperature 59.1°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 32.2°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 87.6°F; 43.73 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [27].
- **Safety:** The 2025 Annual Security & Fire Safety Report (issued October 1, 2026; covers 2023–2025) is at https://publicsafety.jhu.edu/assets/uploads/sites/9/2024/11/annual_report_securityfiresafety.pdf [24]. The site serves a bot-protection challenge, so offense counts aren't given here. The report says the Johns Hopkins police department has operated 24 hours a day, 7 days a week at Homewood, Peabody and East Baltimore since January 1, 2026 [24].
- **Student body:** College Scorecard gives 45.1% men and 54.9% women among undergraduates [15]. CDS B1 lists 2,784 men, 3,499 women and 71 of unknown sex among all undergraduates [14].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The school plan costs $3,624 for 2026–27 and is billed automatically. Students with adequate other coverage may waive it, but F-1 and J-1 students may not [7].
- **Services:** Student Health and Well-Being runs the Student Health and Wellness Center (primary care, travel consults, immunizations; visits free for eligible students whatever the insurance), Mental Health Services (individual and group counseling, psychiatric care, crisis support at no cost to students) and a 24/7 TimelyCare line; Student Disability Services handles accommodations [25]. Some descriptions come from older pages, so confirm hours and locations [25].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

Hopkins students and alumni won 27 Fulbright awards in 2025 [8]. Gregg Semenza and William Kaelin (2019 medicine laureates) were the 28th and 29th Nobel winners associated with the university as faculty, fellows or graduates [26]. Established 1876 [8].
