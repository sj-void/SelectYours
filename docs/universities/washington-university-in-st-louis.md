# Washington University in St. Louis

Washington University in St. Louis (WashU) is a private nonprofit, coeducational university in St. Louis, Missouri, with four undergraduate schools: Arts & Sciences, Olin Business School, the Sam Fox School of Design & Visual Arts and the McKelvey School of Engineering [1][16].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **First-years only, and you must ask at application.** WashU says "First-year International students can receive need-based financial aid." International transfer applicants cannot get WashU aid. The need-blind policy excludes international applicants [3]. You must say you want need-based aid when you apply; aid is not available after admission [4].
- **Very few international students are aided.** CDS 2025–26 H6 reports 30 degree-seeking nonresident undergraduates with institutional aid, an average of $71,003 and a total of $2,130,097 [1]. That is about 3.7% of the 810 nonresident undergraduates (our calculation) [1]. The school says it meets 100% of demonstrated need, without loans, for admitted students [4][19].
- **Forms:** CSS Profile (code 6929) for aid applicants. Applicants not seeking aid submit the International Financial Information form. Enrolling students need an Affidavit of Financial Support and certified bank statements [4].
- **Selective for internationals:** In Fall 2025, 575 of 7,830 international applicants were admitted (7.3%) and 275 enrolled (CDS 2025–26 C1) [1]. International students were 13% of the Class of 2030 [15].
- **English test rule changes for Fall 2027:** SAT or ACT scores no longer replace an English proficiency test. Non-native speakers submit TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo unless they qualify for a waiver [5].
- **Early Action is new for Fall 2027** per the admissions news page, with a November 2 deadline [6]. International aid applicants have an aid deadline of Nov 17 for ED I and EA [4]. Pages read set no separate limit on international aid applicants in early rounds [3][4].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-aware for internationals. WashU's need-blind policy leaves out international applicants, transfer students and wait-list admits, but the school says it still meets 100% of need for admitted students [3]. The admissions site says there are no loans in aid offers for admitted undergraduates [19]. First-year internationals can receive need-based aid [3].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - Say at application that you want need-based aid [4].
  - CSS Profile (code 6929); the FAFSA is only for US citizens and permanent residents [4]. CDS H7 also lists the institution's own aid form for nonresidents [1].
  - Aid deadlines: ED I Nov 17, 2026; EA Nov 17, 2026; ED II Jan 11, 2027; RD Feb 1, 2027 [4].
  - If you do not apply for aid, submit the International Financial Information form [4].
- **What a package contains:** The average need-based offer for admitted students is $75,000 (school figure, not specific to internationals) [19]. Package details for internationals (work expectations, summer contribution): Not published on the pages read (checked: https://financialaid.washu.edu/common-questions/).
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 30 degree-seeking nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid; the average award was $71,003 and the total $2,130,097 [1].
- **Rules:** Aid must be reapplied for each year and offers generally stay the same or rise if the CSS Profile is consistent; satisfactory academic progress is required [3]. International transfer applicants are not eligible for WashU aid [3]. Students on student visas cannot work off campus in their first year, and employment should not be treated as a means of support [3]. Outside-scholarship rules: Not published on the pages checked (washu.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Merit scholarships:** International students can be considered for merit-based division scholarships and can apply for Signature Scholar programs, except the John B. Ervin Scholars Program [3]. The Signature Scholar priority deadline is December 16, 2026 [4].
- **Net price calculator:** https://financialaid.washu.edu/costs/cost-calculators/net-price-calculator/ [3]. The cost page says the Instant Net Price Estimator does not apply to international students [8].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App or Coalition Application (both open August 1); WashU questions are in the WashU Pathway; portfolios go through SlideRoom [4]. QuestBridge: WashU takes part, but QuestBridge's National College Match is not open to international students living outside the US [17].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [4]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Aid by | Decision | Reply by |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Early Action | Nov 2, 2026 | Nov 17, 2026 | Dec 23, 2026 | May 1, 2027 |
  | Early Decision I | Nov 2, 2026 | Nov 17, 2026 | Dec 11, 2026 | Jan 4, 2027 |
  | Early Decision II | Jan 4, 2027 | about Jan 11, 2027 | Feb 12, 2027 | Mar 1, 2027 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 4, 2027 | Feb 1, 2027 | Apr 1, 2027 | May 1, 2027 |

- **Fee and fee waivers:** $75, nonrefundable; waivers are available for aid applicants and families facing hardship [4].
- **Required parts:** the Common App or Coalition essay; a 250-word response on your intended field of study; school report and official transcript; one teacher evaluation and a counselor recommendation; the activities list; a portfolio for the College of Art (strongly encouraged for Architecture). Optional: test scores, a video supplement, Schoolhouse.world certifications [4][6]. The optional writing supplement was dropped for Fall 2027 [6]. The mid-year report: Not published on the pages checked.
- **English proficiency:** Required if English is not your first language: TOEFL (including Home Edition), IELTS Academic or Duolingo. The most competitive applicants score TOEFL 100 (old scale, exams before January 21, 2026) or 5.0 (new scale), IELTS 7.0 and Duolingo 130; no hard minimum is stated on that page [5]. The international student office lists minimums of IELTS 6.5 and Duolingo 125 for university-wide admission [22]. Waivers apply to students with three or more years in an English-medium program or US high school, with strong grades; others are case by case. Scores must come from the testing agency; super-scoring is not accepted [5][22].
- **International documents:** Original or certified transcripts, a description of the grading system, school-leaving certificates and certified English translations. Choose one: SAT/ACT (optional), national exam results (predicted or actual), IB, A-levels or three AP scores [4].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** "More than 100 fields of study" [16]. Number of majors and minors and curriculum style: Not published on the pages checked (washu.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** engineering 14%, biological and biomedical sciences 13%, social sciences 13%, interdisciplinary studies 10%, business and marketing 10% [29].
- **Teaching:** Student-to-faculty ratio 10 to 1 (CDS 2025–26 I2: 11,844 students, 1,199 faculty) [1]. CDS I3: 501 class sections of 2–9 students, 743 of 10–19, 264 of 20–29, and 59 of 100 or more, out of 1,892 sections with undergraduates [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering through McKelvey; a Joint Degree Program in Business and Computer Science; PreHealth advising from the first year. Pre-law advising and 3-2 programs: Not published on the pages checked [16][19].
- **Opportunities:** Undergraduate research and study abroad are listed, without participation figures [16]. The CDS marks cross-registration as available [1]. Honors programs: Not published on the pages checked.
- **STEM majors:** OISS says the STEM OPT extension exists (category (c)(03)(C)) but the pages read give no list of eligible majors; ask OISS or check CIP codes [12].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 95% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,747 of 1,842 Fall 2024 entrants) [1]. Six-year graduation rate 93% for the Fall 2019 cohort; four-year rate 85.3% (1,469 of 1,723; our calculation from the CDS grid) [1].
- **After graduation (Class of 2025):** 94% of those seeking work or graduate study reported a positive outcome, and the average starting salary was $89,475 [27]. Top employers and the graduate-school share are in the Center for Career Engagement's dashboard, which doesn't extract as text [20].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings 10 years after entry are $91,497 [24]. These cover federal-aid recipients only, so they exclude internationals.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 810 nonresident degree-seeking undergraduates (10.3%) (CDS B2) [1]. 13% of the Class of 2030 is international; the class comes from 48 states and 29 countries [15].
- **Support:** The Office for International Students & Scholars (OISS) advises on immigration, issues I-20s, and runs international student orientation and check-in [9]. New students request an I-20 in MyOISS after admission, uploading a passport copy, proof of admission, an Affidavit of Financial Support with funding proof for one year, and a verified English test score; OISS processes requests in 3 business days [10].
- **Work:**
  - Most work, paid or unpaid, needs pre-approval; starting work without it violates status [13]. First-year students on student visas cannot work off campus [3]. On-campus work: up to 20 hours a week during fall and spring semesters (some WashU pages say 19.6), counted across all jobs, and full time during breaks. It must be pre-authorized by OISS. F-1 students can't hold Federal Work-Study jobs, and CPT usually starts only after two semesters [28].
  - CPT: after at least one academic year in full-time status, only for work required for the degree, with a cooperative employer agreement. 365 days or more of full-time CPT ends OPT eligibility at that degree level. OISS notes that a court injunction is blocking August 2026 CPT guidance, so check with OISS [11].
  - Post-completion OPT: 12 months [12]. STEM extension: see section 7.
- **Practicalities:** Break housing: The 2025–26 housing terms let students occupy their rooms during Thanksgiving, fall, winter and spring breaks, although the contract period itself excludes the winter break [30]. Mentor programs: Student Transitions & Engagement runs a peer mentoring program for new students [33]. Student organizations: Not published on the pages checked (washu.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** 100% of first-time first-years and 71% of all undergraduates live in college-owned or affiliated housing (CDS 2025–26 F1) [1]. The residential life site lists housing for first-year, sophomore and upper-division students but does not state a residency requirement or guarantee [21].
- **Location:** St. Louis, Missouri [1]. Airport distance: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at St Louis Sci Ctr, MO, 3.9 km from campus: average temperature 58.0°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 27.6°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 88.0°F; 43.91 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [25].
- **Safety:** The 2026 Annual Security Report & Fire Safety Report is at https://police.wustl.edu/app/uploads/2026/09/2026-Clery-ASR-Website.pdf; Danforth Campus crime statistics are on p. 44 [14]. The table extracted unreliably, so no counts are given here.
- **Student body:** CDS B1 lists 3,620 men, 4,497 women and 36 of unknown sex among all undergraduates [1]. 84% of first-years and 85% of undergraduates are from out of state, excluding internationals (CDS F1) [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The WashU Student Health Insurance Plan is billed in the fall and covers 12 months; students can opt out with other adequate coverage by September 5 [8]. 2026–27 premium: $2,923. International students on the Danforth Campus must buy the plan; the only exception is proof of US insurance through a US employer via a parent or spouse. Waivers now go through the Care26 platform [26].
- **Services:** Student health and wellness fee of $700 is part of 2026–27 charges [8]. Counseling: the Center for Counseling & Psychological Services runs free, confidential "Let's Talk" same-day drop-in consultations [32]. Health center and disability services details: Not published on the pages checked (washu.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

Distinctive programs include the Joint Degree Program in Business and Computer Science and the Beyond Boundaries division [15][19]. Notable alumni: Not published on the official pages checked (washu.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
