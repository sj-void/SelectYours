# University of Rochester

The University of Rochester is a private nonprofit research university in Rochester, New York. Undergraduates study mainly on the River Campus; the Eastman School of Music is on a separate campus downtown [1][17].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-aware, with very limited need-based aid.** Financial need is a factor in admission for internationals, so admission is "highly selective for students with financial need." Rochester says it meets full demonstrated need for admitted students regardless of citizenship, but only a small number of international applicants will qualify for need-based aid [3].
- **Prove you can pay if you don't get aid.** Applicants not seeking need-based aid must submit the International Financial Support Form (IFSF), and the application is incomplete without it. Applicants who cannot secure the needed funding cannot be admitted [3].
- **Aid applicants file the CSS Profile (code 2928)** by Nov 1 (ED) or Jan 5 (ED II, RD) for Fall 2027. International students do not file the FAFSA; some must also send documents through IDOC [9].
- **Merit scholarships are automatic.** The Meliora Scholarship ($10,000–$35,000, average about $20,000) and other awards consider every admitted applicant; the international admissions page says merit scholarships are open to all applicants [3][12]. CDS H6 shows 510 nonresident undergraduates with institutional aid averaging $40,057, but it combines need-based and merit aid [1].
- **Interviews are optional but "highly recommended,"** and international applicants can use alumni, Zoom or the third-party InitialView option [7][13].
- **International students are 14% of the Class of 2030**, from 33 countries [8].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Enrollment:** 6,096 undergraduates and 11,211 students in total (CDS 2025–26 B1, Fall 2025) [1]. The university facts page lists 5,633 full-time and 463 part-time undergraduates and 4,673 graduate students for 2025–26 [8].
- **Campus and calendar:** The River Campus is the main undergraduate campus; Eastman is downtown [17]. The River Campus covers 154 acres, about two miles south of downtown on a bend of the Genesee River [29]. Founding year and academic calendar: Not published on the pages checked (rochester.edu, searched 2026-11-23). Nearest airport: The River Campus is about two miles from Frederick Douglass Greater Rochester International (ROC), about 10 minutes by Uber or Lyft; the university runs no airport shuttle [35].
- **Schools:** Rochester students major in humanities, social sciences, natural sciences or engineering, and the Eastman School of Music offers a bachelor of music [7][14].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-aware for internationals. "Financial need is a factor" in admission decisions for them; the university says it meets full demonstrated need for admitted students without regard to citizenship, but "only a small number of international applicants" qualify for need-based aid [3]. International students are eligible for both need-based aid and merit scholarships [4][9].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - CSS Profile (code 2928). First-year deadlines (midnight ET): ED I Nov 1, 2026; ED II and RD Jan 5, 2027; transfer March 15 (fall) [9].
  - IDOC: some international students must send institutional forms and tax information through the College Board [9].
  - IFSF: for applicants who do not need need-based aid; required to complete the application [3].
  - CSS Profile fee waivers are limited and need a submitted Common App or Coalition application; requests are due Jan 15 (first-year) [3][4].
- **What a package contains:** Not stated on the international aid page (grant, loan and work breakdown) (checked: https://www.rochester.edu/financial-aid/international-undergraduates/). ED aid packages are emailed in late December, 5–10 business days after the decision [5].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 510 degree-seeking nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid (need-based or non-need-based); the average was $40,057 and the total $20,428,748 [1]. H6 does not separate need-based from merit awards.
- **Rules (2025–26 Financial Aid Handbook):**
  - Renewal applications are due March 15 of the prior year. Renewals still incomplete after June 1 lose $2,000 of Rochester National Grant.
  - Outside scholarships replace self-help first; any excess reduces university grant or scholarship. Total awards can't exceed need or the cost of attendance [30].
  - Whether aid can be requested after admission, and international transfer aid: Not published on the pages checked. The aid page lists a March 15 transfer CSS deadline for fall [9].
- **Merit scholarships:** All admitted undergraduates are considered. The Meliora Scholarship is $10,000–$35,000 (average about $20,000). The Alan and Jane Handler Scholarship gives full support to students with substantial need, by nomination. The scholarships page does not address international eligibility; the international admissions page says merit scholarships are open to all applicants with no extra documents [3][12]. The priority scholarship deadline is Dec 1 [5].
- **Net price calculator:** https://npc.collegeboard.org/app/Rochester. It "will not provide accurate results" for students who are not US citizens or eligible non-citizens [10].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App or Coalition (Scoir); Eastman bachelor of music applicants apply directly through Eastman [7]. QuestBridge: not mentioned on the first-year application page [7].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [5]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Decision | Reply |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Early Decision I | Nov 1, 2026 | Mid-December | Within 3 weeks |
  | Priority scholarship | Dec 1, 2026 | Late February | May 1, 2027 |
  | Early Decision II | Jan 5, 2027 | Early February | Within 3 weeks |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 5, 2027 | April 1, 2027 | May 1, 2027 |

  Supplemental materials are due Nov 25 (ED I), Jan 15 (ED II) and Feb 5 (RD). The page does not label the dates as Fall 2027, but they are the current first-year cycle [5].
- **Fee and fee waivers:** $50; a fee waiver request form is available for applicants with significant financial burden [7].
- **Required parts:** the university-specific questions with a supplemental essay (the university discourages AI-written essays); a School Report with counselor recommendation; one teacher recommendation (up to three allowed); the mid-year report; and the IFSF or CSS Profile. Optional: a Family/Peer Recommendation Form and media links such as portfolios [3][7].
- **English proficiency:** Duolingo, IELTS or TOEFL iBT (code 2928). Recommended scores: Duolingo 130, IELTS 7.5, TOEFL 100 (old scale) or 5 (new scale, from January 21, 2026). These are recommendations, not hard minimums; applicants below them may be admitted, possibly with the English for Academic Purposes Program. Sub-scores are not combined across sittings and TOEFL MyBest is not accepted. Waivers cover native speakers and applicants with three years in English-instruction high schools [3].
- **International documents:** Secondary school transcript, final transcript, and documents sent directly by the school, testing site or family member. The testing page lists accepted national exams (for example Gaokao, A-levels, Abitur, IB-related options); IB applicants send a predicted-scores letter from the IB coordinator. Official scores are due before enrollment [3][6].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** "More than 160 undergraduate academic programs" [15]. Most popular fields (CDS 2025–26 J1, share of degrees): biological and life sciences 12.76%, health professions 12.31%, business 11.22%, social sciences 10.17%, engineering 9.82%, psychology 9.47%, computer and information sciences 7.78% [1]. The Rochester Curriculum is open: no general education requirements and no core, a Primary Writing Requirement, and clusters of three related courses in each of the two areas outside the major [14].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** biological and biomedical sciences 13%, health professions 12%, business and marketing 11%, social sciences 10%, engineering 10% [32].
- **Teaching:** Student-to-faculty ratio 9.7 to 1 (CDS I2: 7,597 students, 787 faculty). CDS I3: of 1,199 class sections with undergraduates, 396 have 2–9 students, 440 have 10–19, 119 have 20–29, and 27 have 100 or more [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering is one of the four broad areas of study [14]. Combined degree programs allow one application to yield two admission offers (a combined undergraduate and graduate degree, or degrees from two schools) [14]. Pre-health and pre-law: the career center reports a 72% acceptance rate to MD and DO programs over three cycles for students using its health committee process, and a 77% average admit rate for law over the 2020–21 to 2024–25 cycles [26].
- **Opportunities:** More than 75% of undergraduates take part in research [8]; study abroad covers 40+ countries with 25% participating in 2025–26 (a separate page says programs span 70+ countries) [8][16]; more than 275 student organizations [14].
- **STEM majors:** STEM OPT gives 24 extra months for a qualifying STEM degree; the list is the ICE STEM Designated Degree Programs List, checked against the CIP code on your I-20 [22].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 90.7% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,187 of 1,309 Fall 2024 entrants) [1]. Six-year graduation rate 83.4% for the Fall 2019 cohort (1,243 of 1,491) [1]. College Scorecard gives 75.2% within 4 years and 89.6% within 6 years for nonresident students [27].
- **After graduation:** The admissions site reports 47% of graduates employed and 45% in further study, without naming a class year [31]. Class of 2025: 94% of Computer Science graduates had a job or graduate place [31]. The career center's dashboard (employers, graduate schools) is interactive and doesn't extract as text [26].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings 10 years after entry are $88,358 [27]. These cover federal-aid recipients only, so they exclude internationals.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 1,212 nonresident degree-seeking undergraduates (20.6%) (CDS B2) [1]; 14% of the Class of 2030 are non-US citizens, from 33 countries [8].
- **Support:** The International Services Office (ISO), part of the Office for Global Engagement, handles visa and travel guidance, employment compliance, new student processing and welcome events [18]. New students request an I-20 through the New Student Information Form (NSIF) after admission; then pay the I-901 fee, get a visa, and complete Immigration Check-In in URcompass within a week of arriving. Arrival is allowed up to 30 days before the program start date [19].
- **Work:**
  - On campus: up to 20 hours a week in term and full-time in official vacations [20].
  - CPT and OPT: ISO lists CPT, pre- and post-completion OPT and STEM OPT; OPT is tied to the current degree level [21].
  - STEM OPT: 24 months after 12 months of OPT, with Form I-983 [22].
- **Practicalities:** Break housing: Undergraduates with fall 2026 housing and a spring 2027 contract can stay over winter break by registering in StarRez by December 14; the fee is $400, with need-based help available [34]. Mentor programs: International Student Mentors (upper-class students, each matched with about 20 newcomers) contact new international students in the summer and stay in touch through the first year; the program runs through the College Center for Advising Services [36].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** First-year students must live on campus, mostly in doubles; upper-year students use a March housing lottery and may live off campus [17]. CDS F1: 98% of first-years and 71% of undergraduates live in college housing [1]. The admissions campus page says about 80% of undergraduates; we report the CDS figure and note the difference [17].
- **Location:** Rochester, New York [17]. Airport distance: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Rochester Gtr International Airport, NY, 3.8 km from campus: average temperature 49.5°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 21.3°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 80.3°F; 35.09 inches of precipitation a year, including 102.0 inches of snowfall [28].
- **Safety:** The 2026 Annual Security and Fire Safety Report is at https://adminfinance.rochester.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-Annual-Security-and-Fire-Safety-Report-University-of-Rochester.pdf. For 2025, on-campus offenses reported were 6 rapes, 13 fondling, 2 robberies, 24 aggravated assaults, 10 burglaries and 115 motor vehicle thefts (including electric scooters); the geography includes Strong Memorial Hospital [25]. The table text extracted imperfectly, so check the PDF.
- **Student body:** CDS B1 lists 2,864 men, 3,200 women and 32 of unknown sex among undergraduates [1]. 55% of first-years are from out of state, excluding internationals [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** University policy requires all full-time students to carry health insurance. Students can enroll in the sponsored plan (through Aetna) or apply to waive it with comparable coverage; the mandatory health fee cannot be waived [23][24]. The 2026–27 premium and waiver deadlines were not on the pages read. An update to the Student Health Insurance Plan took effect August 1, 2026 [24].
- **Services:** University Health Service offers primary care and health promotion; the University Counseling Center provides assessments and treatment plans [24]. Disability services: The University Counseling Center (part of University Health Service) gives counseling and keeps a directory of off-campus clinicians. The Office of Disability Resources handles academic, housing and dining accommodations [33].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

Distinctive features: the open Rochester Curriculum, the Eastman School of Music, combined degree programs, and a high share of research participation [8][14]. Notable alumni: Rochester's economics PhD alumni include Nobel laureate Richard Thaler (PhD '74) [37].
