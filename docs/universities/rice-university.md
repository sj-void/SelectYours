# Rice University

Rice is a private nonprofit research university in Houston, Texas, on about 300 acres of wooded grounds. Its charter was filed in 1891 and it opened in 1912 [9].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-aware for admission and aid, and limited.** Rice offers need-based aid to "a limited number of international first-year applicants." The admission committee considers a family's likely aid need. You must request aid on the International Student Financial Statement; if you are admitted without asking, you cannot apply in later years [3][6].
- **Full need met with grants.** International students who receive need-based aid "will have 100% of demonstrated need met with institutional grant aid." They are not eligible for the Rice Investment [3].
- **Actual aid (CDS H6):** 164 international degree-seeking undergraduates received institutional aid in Fall 2025, averaging $79,444 (total $13,028,832) [1].
- **Very selective for internationals.** In Fall 2025, 332 of 8,791 international first-year applicants were admitted (3.8%) and 164 enrolled (CDS C1 residency breakdown) [1].
- **No application fee waiver for internationals.** The fee is $75 [6].
- **International Student Financial Statement is required for everyone,** with bank statements or affidavits (tax documents and proof of income are not accepted). If your available funds are below $101,634 you must explain how you will cover costs [6].
- **QuestBridge is mostly closed to internationals.** The National College Match is open to US citizens and permanent residents, or to students of any citizenship attending high school in the United States [6].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded:** State charter filed in 1891; opened in 1912 [9].
- **Campus:** About 300 acres in Houston, Texas, at 6100 Main St. [9].
- **Enrollment (Fall 2025):** 4,807 undergraduates (4,716 full-time, 91 part-time) and 4,264 graduate students; 9,071 in total (CDS B1) [1]. Rice's overview page lists 4,776 undergraduates and 4,104 graduate students [9].
- **Schools:** Nine: the George R. Brown School of Engineering and Computing, Jones Graduate School of Business, School of Architecture, School of Humanities and Arts, School of Social Sciences, Glasscock School of Continuing Studies, Shepherd School of Music, Virani Undergraduate School of Business and Wiess School of Natural Sciences [9]. Undergraduate study is organized in seven divisions: architecture, business, engineering and computing, humanities and arts, music, natural sciences and social sciences [8].
- **Residential colleges:** 12; newly admitted students are randomly assigned to one and remain in it [12].
- **Calendar:** semesters. In 2026–27, fall classes run August 24 to December 4, 2026, and spring classes January 11 to April 23, 2027 [19].
- **Nearest airport:** Hobby (HOU) is the closest airport, about 30 minutes' drive; Bush Intercontinental (IAH) is the other [27].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** "Need-aware for both admission and aid." The admission committee considers likely aid need. "International students who receive need-based aid will have 100% of demonstrated need met with institutional grant aid" [3]. They are not eligible for the Rice Investment [3].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - Indicate aid interest on the International Student Financial Statement, which is required for all international applicants [3][6].
  - CSS Profile (school code 6609), with 2025 income and tax information and current assets. Custodial and non-custodial parents each file one. Due Nov 15 (ED I), Jan 4 (ED II), Feb 1 (RD) [3].
  - Tax documents are uploaded through the applicant portal, not IDOC; foreign documents need official English translations [3].
  - Aid awards: mid-December (ED I), mid-February (ED II), April 1 (RD) [3].
  - ISFAA: not mentioned by name [3].
- **What a package contains:** Institutional grant aid [3]. Loans, work and student contribution for internationals: Not published on the page we read. The cost-of-attendance page shows an "Average Freshman Aid Package" of $75,643 and an "Average Responsibility for Families Receiving Aid" of $16,819, without saying whether these include internationals or which year [5]. Rice's admissions home page calls it a "loan-free institution" [13].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 164 international degree-seeking undergraduates received institutional aid; average $79,444; total $13,028,832. Both need-based and non-need-based institutional aid are available to nonresidents [1].
- **Rules:**
  - Students admitted without requesting aid cannot apply in later years [3][6].
  - Renewal and outside scholarships: Not published on the page we read [3].
  - Transfers: no need-based or merit aid for international transfer applicants, at review or after admission [3].
- **Merit scholarships:** No separate application; admitted students are automatically considered and about 20% receive one, notified at admission [6]. The financial aid page does not describe merit awards for internationals [3].
- **Net price calculator:** none for internationals on the pages checked. The cost page does not link one for internationals [5].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App with the Rice writing supplement; QuestBridge National College Match. The Rice page does not mention Coalition [4]. QuestBridge is open to US citizens and permanent residents, and to students of any citizenship currently attending high school in the US; international students at schools abroad appear ineligible [6].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [4][6]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Aid (CSS Profile) | Decision | Deposit |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Early Decision I | Nov 1, 2026 | Nov 15, 2026 [3] | Mid-December 2026 | Jan 4, 2027 |
  | Early Decision II | Jan 4, 2027 | Jan 4, 2027 [3] | Late January 2027 | Mar 1, 2027 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 4, 2027 | Feb 1, 2027 [3] | By April 1, 2027 | May 1, 2027 |

  Shepherd School of Music: all materials due Dec 1 [4]. InitialView interview deadlines for internationals: Nov 10 (ED I), Jan 12 (ED II and RD) [6].
- **Fee and fee waivers:** $75, non-refundable. "International applicants are not eligible to request an application fee waiver" [6].
- **Required parts:** Common App with the personal essay; Rice supplement of two 150-word prompts (academic interests; why Rice) and one 500-word community prompt (choice of two); one counselor letter and two teacher letters; transcripts through an official channel (no email attachments); mid-year grades for RD applicants, and first-marking-period grades encouraged for ED applicants. Optional: "The Box" image, supplemental letters, and portfolios for architecture and art. Architecture needs two 250-word essays. A course profile of 16 units is expected: 4 English, 3 math, 2 lab science, 2 social studies and 2 of one foreign language (the language requirement is waived for curricula without a language option, such as A-levels) [4][6].
- **English proficiency:** Required unless you completed at least two years of full-time study in an English-language curriculum. TOEFL iBT 100 for tests taken before January 21, 2026, or 5.0 on the new scale from that date (code 6609); IELTS Academic 7.0 (General Training not accepted); Cambridge C1 Advanced or C2 Proficiency 185; Duolingo 130. Scores must come from a single sitting; myBestScores is not accepted [6].
- **International documents:** Transcripts for grades 9–11 plus 12th-grade courses, with a certified English translation alongside the original. Exam-based curricula (IGCSE/GCSE, CBSE and similar) need all transcripts, final exam results and predicted results where available. Predicted IB or A-level results must be followed by finals before enrollment. IB Higher Level scores of 6 or 7 and A-level grades A or B can earn credit [6].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** More than 50 undergraduate majors across seven divisions. Distribution requirements are flexible [8]. Engineering students make up 36% of undergraduates. Rice's social sciences division names Economics and Psychological Sciences among the five most popular majors (an older page) [21]. Number of minors: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** biological and biomedical sciences 16%, engineering 16%, computer and information sciences 15%, social sciences 10%, business and marketing 7% [25].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 5.61 to 1 (CDS 2025–26 I2: 4,746.3 FTE students, 846.7 FTE faculty) [1]; Rice cites 6:1 [8]. Class sections with undergraduates (CDS I3): 1,132 in total: 281 with 2–9 students, 416 with 10–19, 168 with 20–29, 89 with 30–39, 64 with 40–49, 89 with 50–99 and 25 with 100+ [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering and computing at the George R. Brown School; separate schools of architecture, music and business [9]. The Office of Academic Advising gives specialist pre-health (hpa@rice.edu), pre-law and pre-graduate advising. Rice has no pre-law major or minor [22]. 3-2 programs: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Opportunities:** Students can begin research "from your first year" [8]. Study abroad: Rice's admission blog says almost half of students study abroad, with 300+ options through the Office of Study Abroad [23]. Cross-registration and honors: Not published on the pages checked.
- **STEM majors:** Rice's OISS says a major qualifies for the 24-month STEM OPT extension if the six-digit CIP code on your I-20 appears on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List. The CIP codes are in Rice's General Announcements (ga.rice.edu/programs-study) [10].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 96.8% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,107 of 1,144 Fall 2024 entrants) [1]. Graduation (CDS B, Fall 2019 cohort, our calculation from the counts): 88.4% in 4 years (848 of 959); 95.3% within 6 years (914 of 959, stated rate) [1]. The CDS table is split by Pell status, not by citizenship [1].
- **After graduation (Classes of 2023–2025 combined, 3,077 bachelor-level graduates; knowledge rate 95% or higher):** 53% working (including ventures, mission-driven organizations, fellowships and military), 33% continuing education, 2% other intentions, 8% seeking employment, 4% seeking education. Top employers and graduate schools appear only as logos [14]. The data are not split by citizenship.
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings are $90,773 ten years after entry and $91,527 six years after entry. These cover federal-aid recipients only and leave out international students [11].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 13.4% of degree-seeking undergraduates are nonresidents (CDS B2); College Scorecard says 12.8% [1][11]. The Class of 2029 is 14% international, from 56 countries. Top countries: China (70), India (8), Brazil (8), Canada (6) and Pakistan (5) (school figures) [24].
- **Support:** The Office of International Students and Scholars (OISS) offers walk-in advising, online forms for CPT, OPT and STEM OPT, and document pick-up in Lovett Hall. New students complete a mandatory pre-arrival course and form, then immigration check-in and orientation after arrival [10][10a]. You may enter the US up to 30 days before the I-20 start date. International first-years pay a $395 international orientation fee [5][10b]. The I-20 issuing process itself: Not published on the pages checked (rice.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Work:** F-1 students may work on campus up to 20 hours a week in term (the limit is weekly, not averaged) and full-time in breaks. CPT needs a job offer letter, and 12 months of full-time CPT removes OPT eligibility. Pre- and post-completion OPT run up to 12 months each; the 24-month STEM extension applies to STEM-designated programs. All options need a full academic year of full-time study first [10c]. OISS shows a banner that a related federal rule on duration of status was "vacated by court order"; check current guidance with OISS [10].
- **Practicalities:** Break housing: Residential colleges close over winter break. Rice's international office has run a hospitality drive offering low- or no-cost housing to international undergraduates who couldn't go home (2022) [26]. Mentor programs and student organizations: Not published on the pages checked (rice.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** 12 residential colleges; every new student is assigned to one. The page does not state a housing guarantee or years required [12]. 99% of first-years and 59% of undergraduates live in college housing (CDS F1) [1].
- **Location and climate:** Houston, a large city (IPEDS locale 11) [11]. NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Houston Hobby Airport: mean annual temperature 71.1°F (max 79.8, min 62.5) and 55.62 inches of precipitation [15]. Nearest airport and transport: Not published on the pages checked (rice.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Safety:** Rice publishes an Annual Security Report ("Annual Report (Houston Campus and Paris Campuses)") through a Box link on the safety page, https://rice.box.com/s/uyquwn0bwbg8qq1huwcpivya5vhipzr3. The 2026 Annual Fire Safety and Security Report (66 pages) was read in Box's online preview. Houston main campus, 2025, on campus: 9 burglaries, 45 motor-vehicle thefts, 7 rapes, 5 fondling reports, 2 robberies and 24 stalking reports [16].
- **Student body:** Among full-time degree-seeking undergraduates, 2,361 men, 2,343 women and 1 of unknown sex (CDS B1) [1]. 59% of first-years and 57% of undergraduates are from out of state, not counting internationals (CDS F1) [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The OISS pre-arrival page lists annual health insurance fees as an initial expense but states no requirement. Rice's cost page says insurance is billed if comparable coverage is not shown [5][10b]. F-1 and J-1 students must enrol in the Wellfleet international plan or an approved alternative. A waiver using private coverage isn't guaranteed. Students who take no action by the deadlines (August 31, 2026 for fall; January 15, 2027 for spring) are enrolled automatically, and premiums aren't prorated [20]. The plan cost wasn't published on the pages checked. New students must complete Rice's Health Data Form, with immunizations and a recent physical, before registering [10b].
- **Services:** The Counseling Center (wellbeing.rice.edu) schedules by phone, 713-348-3311, with urgent help available 24/7 [17]. Student Health Services provides outpatient care for all students whatever their insurance; visits are covered by student fees, but labs and vaccines may cost extra [20]. Disability services: Not published on the pages checked.

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Distinctive features:** a system of 12 residential colleges [12], and research open to students from their first year [8].
- **Notable alumni:** Rice's humanities alumni include William Broyles Jr., founding editor of Texas Monthly and co-writer of Apollo 13, and neuroscientist David Eagleman [28].

None. Our CSV records Rice as need-aware with full need met and no aid for international transfers. The Rice international aid page says the same [3].
