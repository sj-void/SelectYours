## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind and full need for everyone.** MIT describes itself as need-blind and full-need for all undergraduates, "domestic and international" [7]. It says it is "committed to meeting 100% of demonstrated financial need for international students" [5].
- **Applying for aid does not change the timing.** International and domestic applicants file by the same aid deadlines: Nov 30 for Early Action and Feb 15 for Regular Action [6][4]. Aid applicants file the CSS Profile (code 3514) and upload parental income documents through IDOC [5].
- **Very selective for internationals.** For Fall 2025 entry, 6,926 international students applied and 136 were admitted (about 2.0%, our calculation) [21].
- **Loans are optional.** MIT offers international undergraduates with an MIT Scholarship an optional "Technology Loan" of up to $3,400 (plus up to $2,000 with a cosigner). MIT says it calculates aid so students should not need to borrow [8][7].
- **No Early Decision.** MIT has only non-binding Early Action; you may apply in only one round [2][1].
- **SAT or ACT is required.** An English test is strongly recommended if you have used English for fewer than five years or do not speak it at home or school [3].
- **Awards are large.** In 2025–26, 411 nonresident undergraduates received institutional need-based aid, averaging $81,991 (CDS 2025–26 H6) [1].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded:** Incorporated in 1861; it moved to Cambridge in 1916 and admitted its first students in 1865 [22]. Campus: 168 acres; MIT also lists 26 acres of playing fields [22]. (Sources in 14 and 22 are MIT's "About MIT" and MIT Facts 2025 summary.)
- **Calendar:** Two semesters plus a four-week January term, the Independent Activities Period. The first semester is graded pass/no record [13].
- **Enrollment:** 4,561 undergraduates and 7,255 graduate and professional students, 11,816 in total (CDS 2025–26 B1) [1].
- **Nearest airport:** Boston Logan is the closest airport: about 15 minutes by taxi in normal traffic, or about half an hour by subway with transfers (MIT visitor pages, may be dated) [34].
- **Schools and colleges:** Not published on the pages checked (mit.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-blind for all applicants, domestic and international; MIT commits to meeting 100% of demonstrated need [7]. "Domestic and international status does not affect when you apply or the aid you're offered" (paraphrase of the international page [4]). Admissions says money "doesn't" affect admissions decisions [10].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - CSS Profile with MIT code 3514 plus MIT's supplemental questions [5].
  - Parents' tax returns or income documents, uploaded through the College Board IDOC service; MIT does not accept them directly. If parents live abroad, send their country's tax return (an informal English translation is accepted) [5].
  - Separated or divorced parents each complete a CSS Profile [5].
  - Aid deadlines: Early Action Nov 30 (offers mid-January); Regular Action Feb 15 (offers mid-March) [6].
- **What a package contains:** MIT Scholarship (grant) plus a student contribution from summer savings and a suggested term-time work amount, shown on the offer letter; the pages read give no dollar figures [7]. Optional MIT Technology Loan for international undergraduates holding an MIT Scholarship: up to $3,400 without a cosigner, up to $2,000 more with a creditworthy cosigner; no interest until nine months after leaving, then 7%; repay within 10 years, minimum $50 a month [8].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6, nonresident undergraduates):** 411 received institutional need-based aid; average award $81,991; total $33,698,421 [1].
- **Rules:**
  - Outside scholarships may offset the expected student contribution; the pages read do not say how they affect MIT aid beyond that [7].
  - Late applications: no penalty, but aid must be applied for before the academic year ends (mid-May for most students) [6].
  - Transfers: transfer aid deadline Feb 15 [6]. Whether international transfers get aid: not stated on the pages read [19][6]. (Our CSV records no aid for transfers elsewhere; not checked here.)
  - Renewal rules for aid: Not published on the pages checked (mit.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Merit scholarships:** MIT states its aid is need-based [10]; no merit awards described in the pages read.
- **Net price calculator:** An "Estimate your cost" page exists under MIT's Cost and affordability section (not opened) [7].
- **Affordability figures (all undergraduates):** Families earning under $100,000 with typical assets have a $0 parental contribution; under $200,000, tuition is covered. 58% of full-time undergraduates received an MIT Scholarship in 2025–26 [10][7].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** MIT's own application portal [2]. QuestBridge: MIT's QuestBridge FAQ says international students may apply through QuestBridge [28]. QuestBridge's own rules limit the Match to students at US high schools and US citizens or permanent residents abroad [29].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027:** Early Action: apply by Nov 1, decisions mid-December. Regular Action: apply by Jan 4, decisions mid-March. Reply by May 1. EA applicants take tests before Nov 30; RA before Dec 31 [2][3].
- **Fee and waivers:** $75, or a fee waiver (see MIT's fee-waiver FAQ) [2].
- **Required parts:** MIT application form (biographical information, essays, activities, academics), Secondary School Report with transcript, two teacher recommendations (one math or science, one humanities, social science or language recommended), test scores, mid-year update (February Updates and Notes form). Optional: counselor letter, school profile, supplemental recommendation, creative portfolio through SlideRoom [2].
- **English proficiency:** Strongly recommended if you have used English fewer than five years or don't speak it at home or school. Accepted: TOEFL, IELTS, Duolingo English Test, PTE Academic, Cambridge C1 Advanced or C2 Proficiency [3]. Minimum / recommended:
  - TOEFL taken before Jan 21, 2026: 90 / 100; taken on or after Jan 21, 2026 (1–6 scale): 5 / 5.5
  - IELTS: 7 / 7.5
  - PTE Academic: 65 / 70
  - Cambridge: 185 / 190
  - Duolingo: 120 / 125 [3]
  - Waiver rules: not stated.
- **International documents:** Counselor submits the Secondary School Report; send three years of grades and results of national or external exams (IB, A levels, French Baccalaureate, CBSE and similar). Predicted grades may be sent; final grades are needed from admitted students. MIT does not convert grades or apply country quotas [4].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** 56 majors, 62 minors, plus two certificates [13]. Most popular majors: computing majors are MIT's largest, and Course 6-5 (Electrical Engineering with Computing) is already the third-most chosen major among first-years [30]. Curriculum style: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** computer and information sciences 35%, engineering 27%, mathematics and statistics 10%, interdisciplinary studies 10%, physical sciences 7% [31].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 8 to 1 (CDS 2025–26 I2: 11,379 students, 1,466 faculty). Class sections (CDS I3): 509 of 2–9 students, 287 of 10–19, 137 of 20–29, 53 of 30–39, 43 of 40–49, 92 of 50–99, 41 of 100+ (1,162 in total) [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering and computer science are offered; the Schwarzman College of Computing runs the computing majors [30]. Pre-health advising: MIT Prehealth Advising's committee (COPA), made up of faculty and prehealth staff, writes committee letters; there's a $100 annual credential fee, and MIT submits a committee letter at most twice per student [33]. Pre-law and dual-degree programs: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Opportunities:** Nearly 65% of undergraduates take part in UROP each year; 93% of 2025 graduating seniors reported a UROP; 49% reported an international experience at MIT [13].
- **STEM majors:** MIT publishes no list on the pages checked. Check the International Students Office STEM OPT page and the CIP code on your I-20 [11].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 99% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,083 of 1,098) [1]. Six-year graduation rate 96% for the Fall 2019 cohort (CDS B: 1,054 of 1,101) and 86% in four years (946 of 1,101, our calculation) [1]. College Scorecard gives 95.7% (150% time) [15].
- **After graduation:** 2025 Graduating Student Survey results for undergraduates: Not published on the pages checked. MIT's affordability page cites a $145,820 average starting salary for graduates entering industry, with no year stated [7-related page: https://sfs.mit.edu/undergraduate-students/the-cost-of-attendance/return-on-investment/, snippet only].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings $152,553 ten years after entry and $137,488 six years after entry. These cover federal-aid recipients only and exclude international students [15].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 529 international undergraduates (CDS B2) [1]. In 2025–26 MIT students came from all 50 states and 137 foreign countries (all students) [14]. MIT Facts reports 530 international undergraduates and 2,900 graduate students for 2024–25 [22].
- **Support:** International Students Office (ISO): immigration advising, status maintenance, transition support and advocacy. Pages cover new-student I-20/Certificate of Eligibility requests, financial documentation, pre-arrival checklist and orientation; their details weren't summarized in the pages checked [12].
- **Work:**
  - Work on or off campus, paid or unpaid, needs authorization first [11].
  - On-campus total limited to 20 hours a week in term [11].
  - CPT and pre-completion OPT usually require a full academic year in F-1 status [11].
  - Post-completion OPT: 12 months; STEM OPT extension: 24 months for qualifying STEM degrees, with an employer-signed Form I-983 [11].
- **Student organizations:** The MIT International Students Association has run a mentorship program matching international first-years with upper-class students (page from an earlier year) [35].
- **Break housing and host families:** Not published on the pages checked (mit.edu, searched 2026-11-23). The ISO lists programs on cultural adjustment, health and wellness and professional development [12].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** On-campus housing is guaranteed for four consecutive years; unmarried first-years must live in a residence hall [13][18]. CDS F1: 100% of first-year students and 93% of all undergraduates live in college-owned housing [1].
- **Location:** Cambridge, a midsize city (IPEDS locale 12) beside Boston [15].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Jamaica Plain, MA, 6.7 km from campus: average temperature 51.2°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 22.0°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 82.2°F; 49.36 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [26].
- **Safety:** The 2026 Annual Security and Fire Safety Report was published 2026-09-29 by the MIT Police [17]. Headline crime counts: not given here (the PDF link returned HTTP 404 when re-checked 2026-11-23).
- **Student body:** 4,561 undergraduates; College Scorecard shows 51.8% men and 48.2% women [15]. CDS C1 applicants-by-sex counts: admitted 632 men, 638 women, 64 unknown [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** All students are enrolled in and charged for MIT SHIP unless they waive with comparable coverage that meets Massachusetts rules (US-based carrier). Waivers must be renewed each year; deadlines Aug 15 (fall) and Jan 15 (spring). Premiums are fully funded for undergraduates with family income under $100,000 [16][9]. Whether an international student's home-country plan qualifies: not stated; check with the insurance office.
- **Services:** MIT Health provides medical and mental health care. Massachusetts law requires full health coverage beyond what tuition covers [27]. Counseling and disability services details: Disability and Access Services sets accommodations through an interactive process (managed in the Accommodate system). Student Mental Health & Counseling offers brief treatment, referrals and "Let's Chat" drop-in consultations [32].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Research:** nearly 65% of undergraduates take part in the Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program (UROP) [13].
- **Nobel laureates:** Joshua Angrist's 2021 prize brought MIT's alumni Nobel tally to 39, according to the MIT Alumni Association. Counting faculty and researchers, MIT links about 100 laureates to the Institute [23].
- **Notable alumni:** Kofi Annan (SM 1972), UN Secretary-General and Nobel Peace laureate; astronaut Buzz Aldrin [24]. Victor Ambros and Simon Johnson were MIT alumni among the 2024 laureates [25].
