## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind for first-year internationals.** Brown "will be need-blind for international students beginning with the Class of 2029," and meets full need for admitted students [7]. Students admitted before that stay under the need-aware rules they applied under [9]. Our CSV notes the policy depends on further donor funding; that caveat was not on the pages read [7].
- **You must ask for aid when you apply.** International students who do not apply for and receive aid at admission cannot apply later; international transfers must also apply at admission [9][14]. Only a "limited number of isolated and extreme situations" can be petitioned (transfer page) [14].
- **CSS Profile only.** Brown's code is 3189. The ISFAA and paper CSS Profile are not accepted. Brown gives no CSS fee waiver. Aid deadlines: Nov 2, 2026 (ED), Feb 1, 2027 (RD) [9].
- **No loans, no merit awards.** Brown has removed packaged loans from all undergraduate aid [8]. Aid is need-based only [7].
- **Health insurance is compulsory and international plans are not accepted for waiver.** The 2026–27 plan is $5,283 a year (rate pending state approval) [15].
- **English proof is demanding.** Minimums include TOEFL iBT 5.5 (new scale), IELTS 8.0 and Duolingo 130 [16].
- **Applicants ordinarily resident in Iran:** an OFAC action effective Aug 24, 2026 means Brown may not process their applications or fees [12].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-blind for first-year international applicants from the Class of 2029 on. Brown commits to covering "the full need it determines" for admitted students who apply for aid at admission [7]. Students admitted before that date remain under the need-aware rules [9].
- **Forms and deadlines:** CSS Profile (code 3189), open Oct 1, 2026. Deadlines: ED Nov 2, 2026; RD Feb 1, 2027. If College Board asks, upload through IDOC: parents' 2025 tax returns or non-filer statements, or an employer earnings statement. ISFAA and paper CSS Profile are not accepted. Applicants from Iran and North Korea email financial_aid@brown.edu for an alternative [9]. Certification of Finances: not mentioned [9].
- **What a package contains:** Scholarship with no packaged loans [8]. A work offer of about $2,950 (8–10 hours a week) is an opportunity, not an obligation [20]. Summer earnings expectation: $2,700 for first-years and $2,900 for returning students (2025–26). If you don't work, that amount becomes part of your need [36]. Brown says U.S. rules limit off-campus summer work, so international applicants should plan their own summer funding [7].
- **Actual awards (CDS H6):** Not available, since Brown publishes no CDS file (see the data-gap note). 51% of the Class of 2030 received need-based aid (all students, not just internationals) [8].
- **Rules:**
  - Renewal: not described beyond the rule that need-aware students who did not receive aid at admission cannot apply later [9].
  - Outside scholarships: they first replace the summer earnings expectation and then student employment, but never the parent contribution. Any amount beyond your expected student effort reduces the Brown Scholarship dollar for dollar. In the first year they can only replace summer earnings [36].
  - Transfers: international transfers must apply for and receive aid at admission; CSS deadline for the last cycle was March 1, 2026 [14].
- **Merit scholarships:** None. Aid is need-based only [7].
- **Net price calculator:** College Board calculator https://npc.collegeboard.org/student/app/brown. Brown's page says it is for US citizens, permanent residents, DACA and undocumented students, so it may not cover internationals [8].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common Application with the Brown member section. No separate international application [12]. QuestBridge: the National College Match is open only to students at US high schools (any citizenship) and to US citizens and permanent residents abroad [37].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [13][19]:** ED Nov 1, 2026 (decisions mid-December); RD Jan 5, 2027 (decisions by early April; reply by May 1). ED II: none. Brown has one Early Decision round [13][19].
- **Fee and waivers:** $80. Automatic waivers for students in listed low-income programs; other applicants use a College Board or NACAC waiver or a counselor letter. Whether internationals qualify: not stated [13].
- **Required parts:** four short essays (150–250 words on the Open Curriculum; 150–250 on how your upbringing shaped you; 100–150 on a class you would teach; 100–150 on something that brings you joy). PLME and Brown|RISD applicants write extra essays. Counselor and teacher recommendations come through the Common App. Supporting documents can arrive up to a week after the deadline [13]. Mid-year report: Not published on the pages checked (brown.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **English proficiency:** TOEFL, IELTS, Duolingo, Pearson or Cambridge. Usual minimums: TOEFL iBT 105 (before Jan 2026) or 5.5 (Jan 2026 onward); IELTS 8.0; Duolingo 130; Pearson 75; Cambridge C1/C2 score 191. Testing is "highly recommended" if English is not your first language. Official reports are due in May 2027 for self-reporters. MyBest scores are not accepted. Brown offers no ESL for undergraduates [16].
- **International documents:** documents must be in English or come with official translations (send the originals too). Submit predicted or actual results for national exams such as A-levels, Abitur, Baccalaureate or IB; actual scores are expected before matriculation [21][22].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** "80+" concentrations; Open Curriculum with no required core [5][17]. Minors: Not published on the pages checked (brown.edu, searched 2026-11-23). In engineering, Mechanical (49 juniors) and Biomedical (33) were the largest concentrations in Fall 2025 [38].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 25%, computer and information sciences 16%, biological and biomedical sciences 12%, mathematics and statistics 11%, interdisciplinary studies 6% [43].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 6:1 (admissions page and Brown facts) [5][6]. 69% of undergraduate classes have fewer than 20 students (Class of 2030 page); the facts page gives 68% [5][6]. IPEDS (via College Scorecard) also gives 6:1 [31].
- **Fields:** PLME (4,687 applicants, 50 enrolled) and Brown|RISD Dual Degree (856 applicants, 20 enrolled) [6]. School-reported medical-school and law-school admission rates are both 81% [6]. Engineering: ScB concentrations in Biomedical, Chemical, Computer, Design, Electrical, Environmental, Materials and Mechanical Engineering and Engineering Physics. Several are ABET-accredited [38]. 3-2 programs and advising: Not published on the pages checked.
- **Opportunities:** 82% of undergraduates took part in summer internships, fellowships or research (2021 figure) [23]. Study-abroad participation: Not published on the pages checked (brown.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **STEM majors:** Brown's CIP-code list marks STEM-eligible programs. Examples: Applied Mathematics (27.0301), Cognitive Science (30.2501, ScB), Computational Biology (26.1104, ScB) and Neuroscience (26.1501, ScB) [39]. For undergraduates, OPT has to relate directly to the concentration. From October 1, 2026, ISSS charges a $160 fee for post-completion OPT requests [40].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion (IPEDS via College Scorecard):**
  - First-year retention 98.8% (full-time students).
  - Graduation: 74.8% within 4 years and 95.6% within 6 years; 96.3% within 6 years for nonresident students [31].
- **After graduation:** 96% of graduating seniors went to work or graduate or professional school right away (2021); 80% pursued further education within 10 years of graduating [6][23]. Top employers: Not published on the pages checked (brown.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings 10 years after entry are $99,974 [31]. These cover federal-aid recipients only, so they exclude internationals.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** Students from "122+" nations and territories [5]. 12.7% of degree-seeking undergraduates are nonresidents (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [31].
- **Support:** Office of International Student and Scholar Services (OISSS, Page-Robinson Hall, 3rd floor) handles immigration and work questions. The Global Brown Center runs an International Mentoring Program and a required International Orientation for F-1 and J-1 students (2026: Aug 29 to Sept 1) [24][25]. I-20 process: Not published on the pages checked (brown.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Work:** F-1 students may work on campus up to 20 hours a week in term without separate approval; off-campus work needs prior permission. CPT, OPT and STEM OPT rules were not explained on the page read [26].
- **Practicalities:** The Global Holiday Table matches international students staying over winter break with hosts; the Global Brown Center lists spring-break activities [27]. Break housing: international students in campus housing can apply to stay over winter break for $45 a day, without meals. Residence halls closed December 21, 2025 and reopened January 17, 2026. F-1 students can apply for financial help with this cost [41]. More than 500 student organizations [4].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** All first-year students live on campus and 74% of undergraduates do (school figures) [4]. CDS F1: not available (no CDS file).
- **Location:** College Hill, Providence, about 30 minutes' drive from a beach [4]. Nearest airport: Rhode Island T.F. Green International (PVD); see the climate line below.
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Providence T F Green Airport, RI, 11.8 km from campus: average temperature 52.1°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 24.4°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 81.2°F; 47.54 inches of precipitation a year, including 36.6 inches of snowfall [32].
- **Safety:** 2026 Annual Security and Fire Safety Report: https://publicsafety.brown.edu/campus-safety-report-2026. The report says Brown began a Campus Crime Program Review in December 2025 and found corrections were needed to its Clery statistics for 2023–2024. Its 2025 figures are being published for the first time [28]. The statistics table did not extract cleanly, so no counts are quoted here.
- **Student body:** 50.0% men, 50.0% women among undergraduates (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [31]. CDS B2: not available (see the data-gap note).

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** Degree-seeking students are automatically enrolled in the Student Health Insurance Plan (SHIP) unless they get a waiver. 2026–27 annual premium $5,283 (pending approval). Waiver plans must be ACA-compliant and US-underwritten; international plans and travel plans are not accepted. Fall waiver closes Aug 29, 2026 (2026 cycle; next cycle dates not yet posted). J-1 students are not eligible for SHIP [15].
- **Insurance help:** Students who get a need-based Brown scholarship and have no comparable cover can receive extra scholarship for SHIP after a waiver is denied [29].
- **Services:** Student Health and Wellness includes Health Services and Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS) [30]. Disability services: Student Accessibility Services (sas@brown.edu, 401-863-9588) arranges accommodations such as extended exam time, note takers, accessible housing and an accessibility shuttle. You register through Accommodate, upload documentation and meet a specialist. There's no deadline, but accommodations aren't retroactive [42].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Distinctive programs:** the Open Curriculum, PLME and the Brown|RISD Dual Degree (see section 7). 97% of the Class of 2030 was in the top 10% of its graduating class (school figure) [6].
- **Notable alumni:** Brown's 2014 honorary degrees went to nine alumni, including Pulitzer-winning novelist Jeffrey Eugenides and children's author Lois Lowry [44].
