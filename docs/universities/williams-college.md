# Williams College

Williams is a private liberal arts college in Williamstown, Massachusetts. It has about 2,100 undergraduates and a small graduate program in art history and development economics [1][3].

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-aware, but very generous.** Williams says it "isn't need-blind for international applicants". About 70% of international students receive aid, averaging more than $90,000 a year. Awards meet 100% of demonstrated need and are guaranteed for four years [2].
- **Aid covers more than tuition.** International aid includes free textbooks, lab and art supplies, music lessons, storage, and one round-trip plane ticket home each year [2].
- **All grants.** Since Fall 2022, packages have had no loans and no required campus or summer jobs; Williams replaced them with grants dollar for dollar (CDS 2025–26, search index) [3].
- **Say yes to aid on the application, or lose it for good.** Any international applicant who might ever need aid must answer "Yes" to the aid question on the admission application. Don't send aid documents with the application. Williams sends instructions with the admission offer and makes an award within seven days of a complete aid application [2][4].
- **No interviews.** You may send an InitialView interview if you have one [4].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded:** Fifteen scholars were admitted to the free school in Williamstown on 26 October 1791. Massachusetts chartered Williams College on 22 June 1793 [7].
- **Campus:** a contiguous core campus of over 450 acres, plus 2,600 acres at Hopkins Forest, the college's nearby research forest [8].
- **Academic calendar:** "4-1-4". Students take four courses in fall, four in spring, and one in January during Winter Study, which is graded pass/fail [9].
- **Enrollment:** 2,076 degree-seeking undergraduates (IPEDS) [1]. The CDS 2025–26 reports 2,101 undergraduates, 49 graduate students and 2,150 in total (search index) [3].
- **International community:** students from more than 100 countries [4].
- **Nearest airport:** Albany International is 40 miles away; Williams runs a bus from Albany Airport for first-year students [24].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-aware. Awards "meet 100% of demonstrated need and are guaranteed for all four years" [2].
- **How to apply:** Answer "Yes" to the aid question on the admission application. Submit the aid application only after admission, following the Office of Student Financial Services' instructions [2]. Admission and aid applications share one deadline: ED Nov 15, RD Jan 4 [2].
- **What a package contains:** Grants only: no loans, no required campus or summer jobs (CDS, search index) [3]. Plus books, supplies, music lessons, storage and one round-trip flight home a year [2].
- **Actual awards:** About 70% of internationals are aided, averaging more than $90,000 a year [2]. CDS H6 counts: Not available. The CDS PDF blocks automated reading [3].
- **Rules:** You must state your aid intent at application to get aid at any point in your four years [2]. Dual US citizens and US permanent residents follow the domestic process [2].
- **Cost calculator:** Williams has no calculator for international students at this time [5].

## 6. How to apply

- **Process:** International applicants follow the same procedures as US first-year applicants, with no additional forms or tests [4].
- **Deadlines:** ED Nov 15, RD Jan 4; transfer Mar 3, 2027 [2].
- **English proficiency and testing:** See section 3 [4][6].
- **Displaced students:** Williams belongs to the Global Student Haven Initiative for students displaced by war, conflict or disaster [4].

## 7. Academics

- **Signature teaching:** Tutorials, where two students meet a professor weekly, started in 1988. Williams has said roughly half to two-thirds of students take at least one; the figures differ between editions of its tour guide [10].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 24%, computer and information sciences 11%, visual and performing arts 9%, biological and biomedical sciences 8%, physical sciences 8% [22].
- **Winter Study:** a January term of one pass/fail course, about 6–8 hours a week. It can include travel, study away or Berkshires internships [9].
- **Student–faculty ratio:** 7 to 1, Fall 2025 (CDS 2025–26 I2, search index) [3].
- **Majors and class sizes:** Not published on the pages checked (williams.edu, searched 2026-11-23). The CDS (J and I3) blocks automated reading [3].
- **STEM OPT:** Williams's International Student Services runs a STEM OPT Extension process for graduates in STEM-designated fields [14]. The list of eligible majors was not on the pages checked.

- **Earnings:** College Scorecard reports median earnings 10 years after entry [1]. It covers federal-aid recipients only and excludes international students.
- **First-destination report:** Not published on the pages checked (williams.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** Students come from more than 100 countries; internationals are about 10% of students [4].
- **Office:** International Student Services (ISS) [11].
- **Arrival:** For Fall 2026, new internationals arrived Monday 31 August for the required International Student Orientation, with no early move-in, and completed SEVIS check-in with ISS [15].
- **Work:**
  - CPT is possible after one full academic year, for curriculum-related work, capped at 20 hours a week during term [13].
  - Pre-completion OPT is part-time (20 hours or less) during semesters, and part- or full-time in summer and winter breaks [12].
  - Post-completion OPT can be filed from 90 days before to 60 days after the I-20 end date [11].
  - STEM OPT extension: see [14].
- **Break housing and mentoring:** Not published on the pages checked (iss.williams.edu, searched 2026-11-23).

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** Williams guarantees campus housing for all four years [16]. All enrolled students must live in college housing during fall, Winter Study and spring [17]. A limited number of seniors are released to live off campus; the cap is 125 for 2027–28 [18].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at North Adams Harriman Airport, MA, 3.2 km from campus: average temperature 47.0°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 17.1°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 78.7°F; 42.30 inches of precipitation a year (this station has no snowfall normal) [20].
- **Annual Security Report:** The 2025 Annual Security and Fire Safety Report (published October 2025; crime statistics for 2022–2024) is at https://hub.williams.edu/css/files/2025/09/2025-Annual-Security-Report-for-Calendar-Year-2024-1.pdf [21]. Williams must publish an Annual Security Report under the federal Clery Act.

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The college plan (UnitedHealthcare Choice Plus – Harvard Pilgrim) costs $3,365 for 12 months. To waive, certify comparable coverage by August 1 [26]. Williams has an accessible-education office with published policies [19].
- **Counseling and disability services:** The Office of Accessible Education is the main contact for disability accommodations, which aren't retroactive. Integrative Wellbeing Services offers mental-health assessment and treatment, with same-day appointments for urgent concerns [23].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Strengths:** the tutorial system and Winter Study [9][10]; all-grant aid with free textbooks and a yearly flight home for aided internationals [2]; a four-year housing guarantee [16].
- **Notable alumni:** Composer and lyricist Stephen Sondheim graduated in 1950 [25].
