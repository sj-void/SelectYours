## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind and full need.** Amherst admits internationals without regard to financial need and meets 100% of calculated need for admitted international students who apply for aid [3].
- **No loans initially.** Aid packages for internationals are grants plus on-campus work, with no loans. If a student cannot meet the work expectation, the college may offer a loan in its place, which the student need not accept [9].
- **Aid is widely used.** The admissions page says 82% of international students receive aid, with an average international award of "$84,000+" for 2025–26 [3].
- **Very selective for internationals.** In Fall 2025, 92 of 6,346 international applicants were admitted (1.4%) and 41 enrolled (CDS 2025–26 C1 residency table; re-check) [1].
- **English test required unless waived.** Applicants whose first language is not English must submit TOEFL, IELTS Academic or Duolingo, unless they spent the last two years at a school teaching in English [6].
- **Forms:** CSS Profile (code 3003) plus income and tax documents; bank statements do not count. If the Profile fee is a serious hardship, the ISFAA can be requested through the applicant portal. No CSS fee waivers [3].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded and place:** 1821; campus described as 1,000 acres including a wildlife sanctuary of about 500 acres; Amherst is about 90 minutes from Boston and three hours from New York City [15].
- **Enrollment:** 1,907 undergraduates, no graduate students, counted October 15, 2025 (CDS B1) [2]. Fast Facts gives 1,907 students from 49 states and 76 countries [8].
- **Calendar:** two semesters, with an optional January term [22].
- **Nearest airport:** Bradley International (BDL, Hartford/Springfield), about 55 minutes by car. Shuttle and car services run between Bradley and Amherst [23].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-blind for internationals. Amherst "meets 100% of calculated financial need" of admitted internationals who apply for need-based aid [3]. Transfers are treated the same as first-years: full need, no loans [9].
- **Forms and deadlines:** CSS Profile (code 3003) in the home currency, plus documents verifying income and taxes (bank statements do not count). Both parents complete a Profile if separated. ISFAA is the fallback if the Profile fee causes hardship [3]. Aid deadlines: ED Nov 13, 2026; RD Jan 15, 2027 [5].
- **What a package contains:** Institutional grant plus on-campus work, no loans in the initial package [9]. International students cannot use Federal Work-Study but may hold college jobs [9]. The FAQ gives a 2026–27 summer saving expectation of $1,800 (lower for low-income and independent students), but it is unclear how it applies to internationals; the page says no base student contribution is set for internationals [9]. Aided internationals get extra scholarship covering the school health plan [13].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 204 nonresident undergraduates received institutional aid. The average award was $84,590, and the total $17,256,307 [26]. The school says 82% of international students are aided [3].
- **Rules:** Aid must be reapplied for every year; Amherst says it meets full need whether it rises or falls [9]. Outside scholarships first replace student employment; beyond that they reduce Amherst grant [9]. Whether a student who did not apply at admission can apply later: Not published on the pages checked (amherst.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Merit scholarships:** None; Amherst awards need-based aid only (our CSV records "no merit aid").
- **Net price calculator:** Amherst's cost estimators page says the tools aren't intended for international students [27].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App, Apply Coalition with Scoir, or QuestBridge application, with the Amherst supplement [7]. QuestBridge National College Match is limited to US schools and residents; QuestBridge's National College Match is open only to students attending high school in the US (any citizenship) and to US citizens and permanent residents abroad, so internationals living outside the US can't use it [29].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [5]:** ED Nov 9, 2026 (aid Nov 13), decisions early to mid December. RD Jan 5, 2027 (aid Jan 15), decision by late March, reply by May 1, 2027.
- **Fee and waivers:** $75. Pre-approved waivers come through Common App, Apply Coalition, QuestBridge, or Amherst's QuickPass and A2A programs [7][30]. The pages checked don't restrict QuickPass by citizenship (amherst.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Required parts:** a writing requirement with three options (a short response under 350 words to a quotation, a graded paper, or an A2A essay); counselor recommendation and two teacher recommendations [7].
- **English proficiency:** Required for non-native speakers: TOEFL iBT (including Home Edition), IELTS Academic or Duolingo. Recommended minimums: TOEFL 100, Duolingo 130, IELTS 7.5. Waiver only for applicants whose last two years of secondary school were taught in English [6].
- **International documents:**
  - Non-native English speakers send TOEFL (iBT or Home Edition), IELTS Academic or Duolingo scores, unless they qualify for a waiver [30].
  - Aid applicants file the CSS Profile plus documents that verify annual income and taxes. Bank statements don't count. Translations into English needn't be official [25][31].
  - If the CSS Profile fee is a serious hardship, you can ask to file the free ISFAA instead [31].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** 43 majors; open curriculum with no distribution requirements; students pick from 800+ courses [8].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 20%, biological and biomedical sciences 12%, computer and information sciences 11%, mathematics and statistics 8%, area, ethnic and gender studies 7% [39].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 7:1 and average class size 18 (school Fast Facts) [8]. CDS 2025–26: student–faculty ratio 6.7 to 1 (I2). Of 439 class sections in Fall 2025, 282 (64%) had fewer than 20 students and 1 had 100 or more (I3) [32].
- **Fields:** Computer Science is a major, and STEM majors include Astronomy, Biochemistry/Biophysics, Neuroscience and Statistics [33]. Pre-health advising: Pre-health advising runs through the Loeb Center; the committee process ends with committee letters and 4–5 individual recommendations uploaded to medical schools in late July, and a Careers in Health Professions advising and mentoring program supports students [40]. Engineering, pre-law and 3-2 programs: Not published on the pages checked (amherst.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Opportunities:** Five College Consortium gives access to 6,000+ courses at Smith, Mount Holyoke, Hampshire and UMass Amherst; 45% of students study abroad [8].
- **STEM majors:** The OPT STEM extension is handled by the Office of Immigration Services [10]. Amherst lists 13 STEM OPT-eligible majors: Astronomy, Biochemistry/Biophysics, Biology, Chemistry, Computer Science, Economics, Environmental Studies, Geology, Mathematics, Neuroscience, Physics, Psychology and Statistics. Check the CIP code on your I-20 [33].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 98% (CDS B22: 472 of 480; re-check) [2]. Six-year cohort for 2019 entrants: 435 of 470 graduated (92.6%, our division; the CDS rate line was blank in the extract) [2]. College Scorecard gives 73.2% within 4 years, 92.6% within 6 years and 92.5% within 6 years for nonresident students [19].
- **After graduation:** 96% of the Class of 2025 were employed, in further study, fellowships, volunteering or military within six months; leading industries include healthcare, management consulting, higher education, non-profit and investment management [11]. Class of 2024 knowledge rate 90% [11]. Top employers and graduate-school placements: Not published on the pages checked (amherst.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings 10 years after entry are $95,161 [19]. These cover federal-aid recipients only, so they exclude internationals.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 244 nonresident degree-seeking undergraduates, 12.8% (CDS 2025–26 B2) [21]; 76 countries (Fast Facts) [8].
- **Support:** Office of Immigration Services (director Hanna Bliss, ois@amherst.edu) handles F-1 status, CPT, OPT and STEM OPT [10]. The Center for International Student Engagement and the OIS run monthly pre-arrival meetings and a pre-orientation in late August [10]. The I-20 is issued by the College from May, once the final aid award or a complete certificate of finances is on file [10].
- **Work:** On campus up to 20 hours a week in term and 40 in vacations. CPT needs a qualifying offer and DSO authorization; 12 or more months of full-time CPT removes post-completion OPT. OPT up to 12 months for undergraduates; paid internships need OPT; pre-completion OPT is capped at 20 hours in term [10]. The Career Center ("Loeb Center") keeps an international student resource page and FAQs [10][11].
- **Practicalities:**
  - Winter break: housing over the closure is limited to registered hardship or approved cases. Amherst's policy names F-1 and J-1 international students reviewed by the Office of Immigration Services as a hardship category. You register through the Housing Portal [34].
  - Thanksgiving: "Share a Meal/Share a Family" pairs students staying on campus with staff, faculty and local alumni hosts [35].
  - International student organizations: Not published on the pages checked.

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** 100% of first-years and 94% of all undergraduates live in college housing (CDS 2025–26 F1) [36]. A written housing guarantee wasn't published on the pages checked.
- **Location:** Small town in the Pioneer Valley [15]. Nearest airport: Bradley International (BDL), about 55 minutes away [23].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Amherst, MA, 2.2 km from campus: average temperature 48.2°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 16.7°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 80.9°F; 46.59 inches of precipitation a year, including 36.5 inches of snowfall [20].
- **Safety:** Annual Security and Fire Safety Report. The 2025 edition is listed on the police page; the 2024 PDF is https://www.amherst.edu/system/files/ASR-Clery%20Report%202024.pdf [12]. Offense counts are in the PDF.
- **Student body:** CDS B1 first-year degree-seeking men 214, women 271 (first-time first-year group) [2]; all undergraduates are 54.9% women and 45.1% men (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [19].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** Massachusetts law requires coverage. The college plan is $3,627 for 2026–27, billed automatically; waive online through Gallagher with an ACA-compliant plan that has providers near campus. Emergency coverage does not extend to the home country. Aided internationals receive extra scholarship covering the plan [13]. No separate waiver rule for internationals was published on the pages checked.
- **Services:**
  - Keefe Health Center, 95 College Street: weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., plus a 24-hour advice nurse by phone (413-542-2267) [37].
  - Center for Counseling & Mental Health: same-day crisis and urgent appointments; a first ongoing-counseling visit usually within 1–2 weeks (413-542-2354) [38].
  - Accessibility Services: arranges accommodations, including requests for assistance animals (accessibility@amherst.edu, 413-542-2337) [37].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **Distinctive features:** an open curriculum, Five College access and a 45% study-abroad rate [8].
- **Notable alumni:**
  - Calvin Coolidge (Class of 1895), the only Amherst graduate to become US president [16].
  - Harlan Fiske Stone (Class of 1894), Chief Justice of the United States from 1941 [17].
  - Five Nobel laureates: Harold Varmus '61 (Medicine, 1989), Henry W. Kendall '50 (Physics, 1990), Joseph Stiglitz '64 (Economics, 2001), Edmund Phelps '55 (Economics, 2006) and Jeffrey C. Hall '67 (Medicine, 2017) [16][18].
