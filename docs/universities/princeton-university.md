# Princeton University

Princeton is a private university in New Jersey. Applicants apply to the university, not to a department, and earn either an A.B. or a B.S.E. across 37 concentrations [9].

## Key points for international applicants

- **Need-blind, full need, grants not loans.** Princeton says admission is need-blind, it meets 100% of demonstrated need with grant aid, and it applies "the same financial aid policy to international students". Grants have replaced loans in aid packages since 2001 [1][2].
- **No CSS Profile.** Princeton does not use the CSS Profile, IDOC or any College Board documents. Everyone files the free Princeton Financial Aid Application (PFAA). FAFSA is only for US citizens and permanent residents [8].
- **Aid deadlines.** PFAA and tax documents: Nov 9, 2026 (Early Action); Feb 1, 2027 (Regular Decision); Mar 9 (transfer) [7][8].
- **International tax documents.** Upload the parents' original tax return (with a certified English translation if needed) covering most of 2025, or an international non-filer statement listing each income source [8]. A non-custodial parent form is needed if parents are divorced or separated, in some cases [8].
- **Cost adjusted for home country.** For families outside the US the expected family contribution is "adjusted for cost of living". For US families it is 25% of income over $150,000 plus 5% of assets over $175,000 [11].
- **Testing is optional this cycle.** Applicants who do not submit an SAT or ACT are not at a disadvantage for Fall 2027. Princeton returns to requiring SAT or ACT scores from the 2027–28 cycle [3].
- **English test.** Needed if English is not your native language and your school did not teach in English (TOEFL, IELTS Academic, Duolingo or PTE Academic). Exempt if you spent at least three years at an English-medium secondary school [1][6].
- **Aid actually given.** 597 international undergraduates received Princeton aid, averaging $86,736 each, or $51.8 million in total (CDS 2025–26 H6) [18].
- **Internationals face much longer odds.** For the Class of 2030, 221 of 11,679 international applicants were admitted, about 1.9%, against 4.1% overall (our calculation from [19]). In Fall 2025 the figure was 241 of 10,567, about 2.3% (CDS 2025–26 C1) [18].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Enrollment:** The enrolled Class of 2030 (as of Sept 4, 2026) is 1,414 first-years, 51% male and 49% female, 12.5% from non-US schools [4]. Degree-seeking undergraduates: 5,709 (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [22]. The CDS B1 totals don't read cleanly from the PDF text layout.
- **Degrees:** A.B. and B.S.E. 37 concentrations, more than 50 minors and interdepartmental certificate programs [9].
- **Academic calendar:** semesters (CDS 2025–26 A4) [18].
- **Founded and campus size:** Founded in 1746 at Elizabeth, New Jersey, as the College of New Jersey, moving to Princeton in 1756. The campus covers about 600 acres per the admissions page (Princeton's research office gives 500) [35]. Princeton says Nassau Hall served as the nation's capital in 1783 [17].
- **Nearest airport:** Newark Liberty is the most convenient international airport: AirTrain to NJ Transit's Northeast Corridor line to Princeton Junction, then the "Dinky" shuttle train to Princeton Station [34].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Need-blind; meets 100% of demonstrated need with grants; no loans in aid packages. Aid is need-based only, with no academic or athletic merit scholarships. International students get the same policy [1][2].
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - Princeton Financial Aid Application (PFAA), free for US and international applicants [1][8].
  - Parents' tax return and W-2s, or non-filer equivalent; for internationals the home-country return (certified translation if not in English) or the international non-filer statement [8].
  - No CSS Profile or IDOC [8]. FAFSA only for US citizens and permanent residents [8].
  - Deadlines: Early Action Nov 9, 2026; Regular Decision Feb 1, 2027; transfer Mar 9, 2027 [7][8]. QuestBridge finalists ranking Princeton: Nov 1 [10].
- **What a package contains:** Grant aid based on a family contribution (US families: 25% of income over $150,000 plus 5% of student and parent assets over $175,000; adjusted for cost of living abroad). Average total family contribution at assets under $175,000: $0 at $150,000 income, $12,500 at $200,000, $25,000 at $250,000 [11]. The pages read do not state a student contribution or summer earnings expectation, and the FAQ lists on- and off-campus student jobs [12].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 597 international undergraduates aided; average award $86,736; total $51,781,584 [18]. Princeton reports 70.5% of full-time beginning undergraduates received grant aid in 2025–26 and 71% of the enrolled Class of 2030 gets aid [4][13].
- **Rules:**
  - Renewal: you must submit a new aid application each year of attendance [12]. Princeton meets full need regardless of citizenship [12].
  - Outside scholarships reduce your Princeton grant; you must report them to Undergraduate Financial Aid [25].
  - Taxes: for students who aren't US tax residents, grant aid above tuition, required fees and books counts as a "nonqualified scholarship". Princeton withholds US tax on it, at 14% for F, J, M and Q visa holders unless a tax treaty applies [26].
  - Late applications and aid for transfers: Not published on the pages checked (princeton.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
  - Study abroad: aid may apply to approved semester or year programs [12].
- **Merit scholarships:** None [2].
- **Net price calculator:** https://puwebp.princeton.edu/financial-aid-public/ui/#/net-price-calculator. It is "designed for families living in the United States and Canada only" and will not give accurate results for families elsewhere [13].

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common Application (Princeton CEEB code 2672) with Princeton-specific questions; QuestBridge. Whether QuestBridge is open to international students is not stated; contact uaquestbridge@princeton.edu [6][10].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [7]:**

  | Round | Application | Arts supplement | Aid (PFAA) | Decision | Reply |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Single-Choice Early Action | Nov 1, 2026 | Nov 6 | Nov 9 | Mid-December | May 1, 2027 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 1, 2027 | Jan 8 | Feb 1 | Late March | May 1, 2027 |

- **Fee and waivers:** $75, waivable for financial need (CDS 2025–26 C13) [18]. Request a waiver through the Common App, or use a Princeton-specific, ACT, College Board or NACAC waiver. Low-income students and US military members qualify for the Princeton waiver without counselor approval [6].
- **Required parts:** Common App essay; Princeton-specific questions (one 250-word academic prompt chosen by intended degree, a 400–500-word lived-experience essay, a 250-word service essay, and three very short answers); a graded written paper in English (O- or A-level coursework is fine); school report, counselor recommendation and transcript; two teacher evaluations from different core subjects; midyear report [6][9b]. Optional arts supplement [6].
- **English proficiency:** TOEFL, IELTS Academic, Duolingo or PTE Academic if English is not your native language and your school did not teach in English; exempt if you spent at least three years at an English-medium secondary school. Minimum scores: Not published on the pages checked (princeton.edu, searched 2026-11-23) [1][6].
- **International documents:** Your record is judged within your own education system. Send internal grades, external exam results (IGCSE, A-level, IB, national leaving exams), or both, with predicted or achieved results if leaving exams are required. No GPA conversion is required. School reports and recommendations may be written in another language but must then be officially translated, and paid translation is not required. The graded paper and teacher comments must be written in English [1].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** 37 concentrations; more than 50 minors and certificate programs. Engineering concentrations: Chemical and Biological; Civil and Environmental; Electrical and Computer; Mechanical and Aerospace; Operations Research and Financial Engineering. Computer science is offered in both degrees [9].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** social sciences 20%, engineering 18%, computer and information sciences 15%, biological and biomedical sciences 10%, public administration and social services 9% [32].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 8 to 1 in Fall 2025 (CDS 2025–26 I2) [18]. Class sizes (CDS 2025–26 I3): of 1,074 class sections, 773 (72%) had fewer than 20 students and 46 had 100 or more [18].
- **Fields:** Engineering and computer science are available as above [9]. Pre-health and pre-law advising and 3-2 programs: Not published on the pages checked (princeton.edu, searched 2026-11-23).
- **Opportunities:** Senior thesis (page not described in detail); credit from more than 100 programs and universities in 40 countries; aid recipients keep their aid on approved study-abroad programs [9][12][15].
- **STEM majors:** Princeton doesn't publish its own list. The Davis International Center points to the federal STEM Designated Degree Program List. Princeton warns it is a very limited E-Verify employer and rarely able to employ students on STEM OPT itself [27].

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** 90.7% of the entering Class of 2026 graduated within four years, and 97.1% of the entering Class of 2024 within six years, both as of June 2026 [19]. First-year retention is 98.4% for full-time students (IPEDS via College Scorecard) [22].
- **After graduation (Class of 2025, Center for Career Development):**
  - Outcomes were recorded for 1,330 of 1,338 graduates (99.4%).
  - By December 31, 2025, 90.2% had secured their next step: 65.3% had accepted a job (including fellowships, military and self-employment) and 23.2% were going to graduate or professional school [23].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard data could not be retrieved (API rate-limited). They would cover only federal-aid recipients and exclude international students.

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** About 740 international undergraduates (12.8%) in 2026–27, from among the 110 nations represented in the student body [19]. International citizens are 12.9% of the enrolled Class of 2030, and 12.5% attended non-US schools [4].
- **Support:**
  - I-20: newly admitted undergraduates get an email within 2 weeks explaining how to complete the Application for Visa Certificate in TigerNav. The Davis International Center reviews it within 5–7 business days and sends the I-20 electronically. Pay the SEVIS fee before entering the US [28].
  - Orientation: Undergraduate International Orientation ran August 18–20, 2026. It's not required but strongly recommended for international first-years [28].
- **Work:** OPT is open to F-1 students. Princeton itself rarely employs students on the STEM OPT extension, since it is a limited E-Verify employer [27]. Students may hold on- and off-campus jobs listed on Princeton's student employment site [12].
- **Mentor programs:** The Fields Center's PUMP pairs each first-year with an upper-class peer mentor (open to all students) [36].
- **Break housing and student organizations:** Not published on the pages checked (princeton.edu, searched 2026-11-23). The housing page doesn't address break housing [16].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** Princeton guarantees on-campus housing for all four years. First- and second-years must live on campus, and nearly all juniors and seniors do. First-years are randomly assigned to one of seven residential colleges: Butler, Forbes, Mathey, Huo, Rockefeller, Whitman or Yeh [16]. CDS 2025–26 F1: 100% of first-years and 94% of undergraduates live in college housing [18].
- **Location:** Princeton, New Jersey; IPEDS locale code 13 (city, small) [22].
- **Climate:** NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Hightstown 2 W, NJ, 12.2 km from campus: average temperature 52.8°F over the year; winter (Dec–Feb) average low 23.6°F; summer (Jun–Aug) average high 84.4°F; 47.45 inches of precipitation a year, including 23.7 inches of snowfall [24].
- **Safety:** The Annual Security and Fire Safety Report 2025 (published October 1, 2025) covers 2022–2024 campus statistics. It's at https://publicsafety.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf2581/files/documents/Security&FireSafety_Report_2025_V5.pdf, with paper copies at 200 Elm Drive [29].
- **Student body:** Enrolled Class of 2030: 51% male, 49% female; 62.2% public school, 17.2% independent day, 11.7% religiously affiliated, 12.5% non-US schools, 8.3% independent boarding; 19% first-generation [4].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** The Student Health Plan is required only if you are not covered by a family policy; 2026–27 fee: $4,220, billed half in fall and half in spring. Undergraduates can waive it each year during the election window (mid-May to June 30), but not with out-of-state HMO, Medicaid, Medicare or Kaiser plans [5][30].
- **Services:**
  - Health Professions Advising: one-on-one advising and committee letters for students heading to health-professions schools [31].
  - Health center, counseling and disability services: The Office of Disability Services coordinates accommodations, with documentation describing functional limits and an intake interview. Counseling and Psychological Services (CPS) is free to enrolled students and offers teletherapy through Uwill [33]. University Health Services runs the Student Health Plan [30].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- **History:** Princeton says Nassau Hall served as the nation's capital in 1783, and that its athletics program is the Ivy League's most successful by titles [17].
- **US presidents:** James Madison (Class of 1771) and Woodrow Wilson (Class of 1879). Wilson was also president of Princeton and governor of New Jersey [20].
- **International:** Pedro Pablo Kuczynski (Class of 1961), elected President of Peru in 2016 [21].
