# Duke University

Duke is a private nonprofit research university in Durham, North Carolina. Its undergraduates enroll in Trinity College of Arts & Sciences or the Pratt School of Engineering [12][14].

**Last verified:** 2026-11-23. **Entry cycle:** Fall 2027 (applying 2026–27). Rules: [README.md](README.md).

## Key points for international applicants

- **Tick the financial aid box when you apply, or you can never get aid.** International applicants who indicate aid interest "are considered in a separate admission pool." Those admitted without aid interest cannot apply for aid in any year [5][6].
- **Wording to read carefully.** The awarding-policy page says Duke admits "a limited number of international students without regard to financial circumstance or aid eligibility," and expects to enroll 20 to 25 internationals a year whose full need is met [4]. The admissions page describes a separate pool for aid applicants [5]. Our CSV records Duke as need-aware (see "Contradictions" below).
- **Aid offers come with the admission letter and are four-year awards.** They renew automatically [5][6]. Federal loans are not available to internationals [6]. International transfer students get no need-based or merit aid [4][6].
- **Actual aid is substantial.** In Fall 2025, 215 international degree-seeking undergraduates received institutional aid, averaging $87,841 (total $18,885,746) (CDS 2025–26 H6) [1].
- **Duke is test-optional** for first-year applicants in 2026–27. English tests are optional, not required [2][3].
- **The compulsory health plan costs extra.** F-1 and J-1 students cannot waive the Duke student medical insurance plan. Students on need-based aid have it covered by grant aid [6][21].

## 1. Snapshot

- **Founded:** 1924, when James B. Duke created the university; its roots go back to Trinity College (1838) [12].
- **Campus:** 8,693 acres in total, including 442 acres on West Campus, 172 on East Campus, 283 on the Medical Campus and 7,044 in Duke Forest [12].
- **Enrollment (Fall 2025):** 6,525 undergraduates, 10,800 graduate and professional students, 17,325 in total [1][12].
- **Schools for undergraduates:** Trinity College of Arts & Sciences (est. 1859) and the Pratt School of Engineering (est. 1939) [12]. The Class of 2030 has 1,786 first-years: 1,428 in Trinity and 358 in Pratt [11].
- **Calendar:** Semesters. Fall 2026 classes begin August 24 and undergraduate classes end December 4; spring classes begin January 6 [22].
- **Nearest airport:** Raleigh-Durham International (RDU), about a 20-minute drive [20].

## 4. Financial aid for international students

- **Policy:** Duke "admits a limited number of international students without regard to financial circumstance or aid eligibility" and expects to enroll 20 to 25 a year whose full demonstrated need is met with a need-based package [4]. Duke's admissions page says internationals who indicate aid interest "are considered in a separate admission pool" [5]. The two statements point in different directions; see "Contradictions" below.
- **Forms and deadlines:**
  - Check "yes" for financial aid interest on the application [5].
  - CSS Profile for 2026–27 (opens October 1), due Nov 3 (early) or Feb 1 (regular) per the admissions aid page [5]. The application page lists additional aid documents due Nov 15 for ED and "CSS Profile and FAFSA" due Feb 1 for RD [2]. The FAFSA is not needed by non-citizens [5].
  - Foreign tax forms converted to US dollars and an English-language statement of income from each parent's employer, with originals and translations. Parents who are unemployed or self-employed can complete a non-filer form [4][5].
  - Certificate of Financial Responsibility (CFR) sent by Admissions on admission. Aid recipients also complete a Foreign National Form and payroll certification [4].
  - ISFAA: not mentioned on the pages we read [4].
- **What a package contains:** Duke does not publish international-specific package details. The page describes need-based grants plus self-help in general terms [4]. Federal loans are not available to international students [6]. Aid recipients automatically get the student medical insurance plan, and the charge is "automatically covered with grant aid" [6].
- **Actual awards (CDS 2025–26 H6):** 215 international degree-seeking undergraduates received institutional aid; average $87,841; total $18,885,746. Duke offers both need-based and non-need-based institutional aid to nonresidents [1]. Nonresident aid applicants must submit the CSS Profile and Duke's international form (H7) [1].
- **Rules:**
  - Four-year awards; they renew automatically, with no annual reapplication [5][4].
  - Students who did not tick the aid box at admission cannot apply later [5][6].
  - Outside scholarships must be reported. Funds beyond tuition, books and fees are federally taxable, and an ITIN is needed to receive scholarship funds [4].
  - Transfers: no need-based or merit aid for international transfer students [4][6].
  - Aid recipients must attend a financial aid session on arrival [5].
- **Merit scholarships:** The Karsh International Scholars Program is open to non-US citizens with demonstrated need. It covers tuition, room and board, fees, any further need and three summers of funding. There is no separate application and no nomination [18]. Duke's merit pages name Robertson and Nakayama (both with separate applications); they do not say whether internationals are eligible [19].
- **Net price calculator:** College Board NPC, https://npc.collegeboard.org/app/duke, and MyinTuition [5]. The page does not say how well they handle international cases.

## 6. How to apply

- **Platforms:** Common App for first-years, or QuestBridge for eligible students. Transfers use the Duke Transfer Application or Coalition [2]. Whether QuestBridge is open to international students: not confirmed on Duke's pages. Duke's QuestBridge partner page says QuestBridge students may apply for aid later "with the exception of international students" [19b].
- **Deadlines for Fall 2027 [2]:**

  | Round | Apply by | Aid documents | Decision |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Early Decision (binding) | Nov 2, 2026 (optional Glimpse video Nov 9) | Nov 15, 2026 | Mid-December 2026 |
  | Regular Decision | Jan 4, 2027 (Glimpse Jan 11) | Feb 1, 2027; mid-year report Feb 15, 2027 | Late March or early April 2027 |

- **Fee and fee waivers:** $85, non-refundable. Waivers are available for qualifying students through the Common App [2]. The page says transfer fee waivers are limited to US citizens, permanent residents and undocumented/DACA students; it does not say the same of first-year waivers [2].
- **Required parts:** the Common App with one personal essay; two Duke short essays of 250 words each (on why Duke and on communities); the transcript, with first-quarter grades for ED applicants and mid-year grades for all; the school report with counselor recommendation; two teacher recommendations. Optional: one letter from an employer or mentor, one arts supplement through SlideRoom, the Glimpse video ($22) and interviews [2].
- **English proficiency:** Not required. Duke is "happy to consider" scores from non-native speakers. Minimums given: TOEFL 100 if taken before January 21, 2026, or 5.0 on the new scale if taken on or after that date; IELTS Academic 7; Duolingo 130; PTE Academic 70; Cambridge C1 Advanced or C2 Proficiency 180. Highest score is used. Duolingo scores are sent officially for free; others may be self-reported. No waiver rule is described [3].
- **International documents:** Transcripts and school reports must come directly from your school. Translations can be done by an official service, an EducationUSA adviser or a school official, and the originals must be submitted with them [3]. Duke's page does not mention national exams (IB, A-levels, CBSE) [3].

## 7. Academics

- **Programs:** 69 majors, 59 minors and 23 certificates; 82% of students pursue more than one major. Students can design their own major through Program II [13]. Duke's top five majors by headcount are Computer Science, Economics, Biology, Public Policy and Psychology [12]. Curriculum style: Trinity is the liberal arts core and every undergraduate takes Trinity classes [13]; a distribution requirement list is in the CDS (E3), covering arts, English, foreign languages, history, humanities, mathematics, philosophy, sciences, social science, intensive writing and PE [1].
- **Most common fields (share of bachelor's degrees, IPEDS via College Scorecard):** interdisciplinary studies 17%, engineering 15%, social sciences 14%, biological and biomedical sciences 12%, computer and information sciences 11% [29].
- **Teaching:** Student–faculty ratio 7 to 1 (CDS 2025–26 I2: 9,263 students, 1,283 faculty) [1]. Class sections with undergraduates (CDS I3): 1,571 in total: 464 with 2–9 students, 651 with 10–19, 178 with 20–29, 113 with 30–39, 47 with 40–49, 76 with 50–99 and 42 with 100+ [1].
- **Fields:** Engineering is offered through Pratt (15.0% of degrees are in engineering per the College Scorecard) [14]. Pre-health and pre-law advising, and 3-2 or dual-degree programs: Not published on the pages we read [13].
- **Opportunities:** 53% of undergraduates graduate with research experience. More than half study away before graduating, with 200+ programs [13]. Cross-registration is offered (CDS) [1]. Honors programs: Not published on the pages we read.
- **STEM majors:** Duke Visa Services says a degree must carry a STEM CIP code to qualify for the 24-month STEM OPT extension. It points students to the DHS STEM Designated Degree list and to the CIP code on the I-20. Economics (A.B. and B.S.) is CIP 45.0603 and eligible [17]. A full list of undergraduate STEM majors: Not published by Duke on the pages we read.

## 8. Outcomes

- **Completion:** First-year retention 97.0% (CDS 2025–26 B22: 1,687 of 1,740 Fall 2024 entrants) [1]. Graduation (CDS B, Fall 2019 cohort, our calculation from the counts): 84.9% in 4 years (1,469 of 1,730); 96.1% within 6 years (1,663 of 1,730) [1]. The CDS table splits cohorts by Pell status, not by citizenship, so there is no nonresident rate [1].
- **After graduation (Class of 2025, Career Center surveys, to six months):** Trinity: 75% employed full-time, 20% continuing education, 4% still seeking; 96% reported employed, in graduate school or in military/volunteer service (90% knowledge rate). Top industries: finance, then computers/IT/technology, then business and consulting. Pratt: 69% employed full-time, 25% continuing education, 4% still seeking; 96% (94.7% knowledge rate). Top industries: engineering/architecture/design, then science or research, then business and consulting [16a][16b]. These reports are not split by citizenship.
- **International-specific outcomes:** Duke Career Center links a "Duke International Undergraduate F-1 OPT Outcomes Data" resource; we could not open it [16].
- **Earnings:** College Scorecard median earnings are $113,937 ten years after entry and $95,364 six years after entry. These cover federal-aid recipients only and leave out international students [14].

## 9. International student life

- **Community:** 11.1% of degree-seeking undergraduates are nonresidents (CDS B2); College Scorecard says 10.5% [1][14]. The top five home countries in Fall 2025 were China, the United Kingdom, India, Canada and South Korea [12]. The Class of 2030 comes from 62 countries outside the US [11].
- **Support:** The Duke International Student Center (DISC) runs "Passport to Duke and Durham & Orientation," including an undergraduate program. It does not advise on immigration. Duke Visa Services (visahelp@duke.edu, (919) 681-8472) handles visas and I-20s [15]. How I-20s are issued: Not published on the pages we read.
- **Work:**
  - On-campus work hours: Not published on the pages we read; contact Duke Visa Services [16].
  - CPT: needs one year of full-time study, a declared major, a job offer and an internship tied to the degree, usually in the summer. Duke currently says it has "temporarily paused CPT for most (though not all) students" after new DHS guidance (no date given) [16][17a]. Twelve months or more of full-time CPT removes OPT eligibility [17a].
  - OPT: up to 12 months, with a 24-month STEM extension for eligible degrees; 90 days of unemployment allowed (150 days in total with STEM extension) [16].
- **Practicalities:** Break housing: winter stays are for students with both fall and spring assignments, by application and a flat fee [30]. Mentor programs and student groups: Not published on the pages checked. The CDS lists an International Student Organization [1].

## 10. Campus, location and safety

- **Housing:** Duke requires undergraduates to live on campus for three years. First-years live on East Campus [23]. 100% of first-years and 85% of undergraduates live in college housing (CDS F1) [1]. A fourth-year housing guarantee: not stated on the first-year page [23].
- **Location and climate:** Durham, in the Raleigh–Durham–Chapel Hill area. NOAA 1991–2020 normals at Raleigh-Durham airport: mean annual temperature 61.2°F (max 72.0, min 50.5) and 46.07 inches of precipitation [24]. Transport: RDU is about 20 minutes by car [20].
- **Safety:** [2026 Annual Security Report](https://forms.hr.duke.edu/media/oarc/2026-Duke-ASR.pdf) [25]. For the Main Campus (including hospital and medical areas), on-campus counts for 2023, 2024 and 2025: rape 3, 2, 9; fondling 5, 11, 6; robbery 1, 1, 1; aggravated assault 4, 2, 5; burglary 23, 20, 25; motor vehicle theft 36, 36, 52; arson 0, 1, 2 [25].
- **Student body:** Among full-time degree-seeking undergraduates, 3,011 men and 3,397 women (CDS B1) [1]. 84% of first-years and 82% of undergraduates are from out of state, not counting internationals (CDS F1) [1].

## 11. Health and wellbeing

- **Insurance:** Students on F-1 or J-1 visas "are not able to opt out" of the Duke student medical insurance plan. The plan is $4,290 for 2026–27 (student only) per the School of Nursing bulletin. Dental is automatic but F-1/J-1 students may opt out [21][26]. Domestic students can waive if their cover meets Duke's criteria [26]. Waiver and enrollment window: June 15 to September 15 (fall) [26].
- **Services:** The mandatory Student Health Fee gives access to Duke Student Health and Counseling & Psychological Services (CAPS). Some services are billed to insurance [21]. Disability accommodations go through the Student Disability Access Office, using the Duke Disability Management System [27].

## 12. Notable alumni and strengths

- Alumnus William G. Kaelin Jr. shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine [28]. Duke's faculty have also won Nobel Prizes (Robert Lefkowitz, 2012 Chemistry; Paul Modrich, 2015 Chemistry) [28].
- Duke has had 50 Rhodes Scholars as of 2019 and 29 Marshall Scholars by 2019 [28].
- Research: about $800 million a year in research spending [13].

## Contradictions with our CSV

Our CSV records Duke as "Need-aware" with full need met. The Duke awarding-policy page says Duke admits a limited number of internationals "without regard to financial circumstance or aid eligibility" [4], which reads like need-blind. The Duke admissions aid page says aid-interested internationals are "considered in a separate admission pool" and that the intake for full-need aid is limited to 20–25 a year [5]. We have not changed the verdict. It needs a decision: the page wording is mixed.
