# Audit of William LeBeau's "Schools That Pay" list

**Page audited:** [williamlebeau.com/courses/study-free-usa-bachelors/schools-that-pay](https://www.williamlebeau.com/courses/study-free-usa-bachelors/schools-that-pay), read 22 November 2026.
**What it claims:** "The Full List of Schools That Meet 100% of Demonstrated Financial Need", with 71 schools. It says the gap is covered with "a grant, not a loan" and that all of them meet full need once a student is admitted.
**How it was checked:** each school's own official website. Full method and sources are in [us-full-need-schools-international.md](us-full-need-schools-international.md).

## Verdict: mostly right, but not fully

**61 of the 71 schools check out.** The other 10 don't hold up as written:

| Problem | Schools |
| --- | --- |
| **Wrong**: no full-need commitment for internationals | Emory University, Union College, Soka University of America |
| **Overstated**: full need only for a few students, or only part of it | Lehigh University, Harvey Mudd College, Thomas Aquinas College |
| **Unclear**: the official pages don't confirm it for internationals | Kenyon College, Scripps College, Case Western Reserve University |
| **Not a bachelor's college** | Deep Springs College (two-year; students transfer) |

**Is it complete?** Almost. I checked 28 other schools, and one qualifies:

- **Rhodes College (TN)** says it "meets demonstrated need for admitted international students on student visas". It is need-aware.

Two more are close but don't fully qualify:

- **Curtis Institute of Music** gives every admitted student a full-tuition scholarship and offers need-based help with living costs.
- **Bard College** guarantees full need only in its early rounds, and caps international aid at tuition.

## Other problems with the page

1. **"A grant, not a loan" isn't true everywhere.** At Cornell, Middlebury, Mount Holyoke, Vassar, Macalester, Claremont McKenna, Franklin & Marshall, Reed, Bryn Mawr, Rochester and Oberlin, packages can include loans. The page is right that many top schools are loan-free.
2. **No need-blind flag.** Only 10 of the 71 are need-blind for internationals: Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Amherst, Dartmouth, Bowdoin, Brown, Notre Dame and Washington & Lee. At the rest, asking for aid can lower your chances. That is the most important difference between the schools, and the list doesn't show it.
3. **Its own count is inconsistent.** The intro says "60+ schools", the heading says "over 70", and the list has 71.
4. **Name error.** "Case Western University" should be Case Western Reserve University.
5. **"COF Required" labels are only partly confirmed.**
   - **Confirmed:** Johns Hopkins, Carleton, Hamilton, Denison and Holy Cross require a Certification of Finances. Holy Cross's was confirmed on 2026-11-23 from its Office of International Students page (https://www.holycross.edu/about-holy-cross/offices-services/office-of-international-students); see [its profile](universities/college-of-the-holy-cross.md).
   - **Not confirmed in the pages I read:** Bryn Mawr and Skidmore.
   - **Not flagged by LeBeau:** Union requires one, and Richmond uses its own Certification of Financial Responsibility.
6. **Missing conditions.**
   - NYU's full-need promise covers only first-years at the New York campus.
   - Brown is need-blind only for first-years.
   - (Corrected 23 Nov 2026: an earlier version said Caltech gives no aid in Early Action. Caltech's international page now allows aid in Restrictive Early Action or Regular Decision.)
   - Wesleyan makes most aided international offers in Regular Decision. (Corrected 23 Nov 2026: an earlier version said it won't admit aid seekers Early Decision. Its page now says it also considers them in ED.)
   - Almost every school refuses aid to students who didn't request it at admission, and most give none to international transfers.

## Schools LeBeau correctly left out

These are often listed as "full need" schools, but they meet full need only for **US** students or give internationals limited aid:

Georgetown, Boston College, Boston University, Northeastern, USC, Carnegie Mellon, UVA, UNC Chapel Hill, Wake Forest, Occidental, Olin, St. Olaf, Bucknell, Dickinson, Gettysburg, Babson, Minerva, College of the Atlantic, Sewanee, Centre, Wabash, St. John's College, University of Miami, College of Wooster and Earlham.

The reason for each is in group E of [us-full-need-schools-international.md](us-full-need-schools-international.md).
