# US colleges that meet 100% of demonstrated need for international students

**Who this is for:** international students (not US citizens or permanent residents) applying as first-years for a bachelor's degree in the USA.
**Last verified:** 22 November 2026, during the Fall 2027 entry cycle (Class of 2031).
**Data file:** [us-full-need-schools-international.csv](us-full-need-schools-international.csv) holds the same research, one row per school.

## Bottom line

**62 US colleges meet the full demonstrated need of the international students they admit:**

- **10 are need-blind for internationals**, so asking for aid does not affect your admission (group A).
- **51 are need-aware**: they meet full need once you're admitted, but asking for aid can make it harder to get in (group B).
- **Berea College** covers the full cost for every international student it admits (group C).

I checked 99 schools in total. The other 37 either fall short of full need for internationals or only partly qualify.

## What counts

A school is listed here only if its **own official website** says it meets 100% (full) of demonstrated need for admitted international students, or for all admitted students regardless of citizenship. Merit scholarships and full-tuition programs on their own don't count.

Terms used below:

- **Demonstrated need:** the school's cost of attendance minus what its formula says your family can pay. The school sets both numbers.
- **Need-blind:** your aid request is not considered when you're admitted or rejected.
- **Need-aware / need-sensitive:** it is. The school has a fixed aid budget for internationals, so a large aid request can lower your chances.
- **Full need is not the same as free, and not the same as no loans.** You still pay the family contribution, and some schools fill part of your need with loans or a campus job.

| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Need-blind for internationals + full need | 10 |

## Rules that apply at almost every school above

1. **Ask for aid when you apply.** Nearly every school says an international student who doesn't request aid at admission can never get it later.
2. **First-year entry only.** Most give little or no aid to international transfer students, for example UChicago, Northwestern, Caltech, Rice, Vanderbilt, WashU, Johns Hopkins, Duke, Claremont McKenna, Barnard, Wesleyan, Vassar and Haverford.
3. **Watch the application round.**
   - Wesleyan considers aid seekers in Early Decision, but makes most aided international offers in Regular Decision.
   - Carleton awards most international aid in Regular Decision.
   - Bard guarantees full need only in its early rounds.
4. **Forms.**
   - Most schools want the CSS Profile.
   - Free alternatives exist at Princeton, Johns Hopkins, Grinnell, Skidmore, Macalester, Carleton, Vanderbilt, Bates, Pitzer, Whitman and Rhodes.
   - A Certification of Finances is required at Johns Hopkins, Carleton, Hamilton and Denison. Richmond uses its own Certification of Financial Responsibility.
5. **Aid is usually set once.** Many schools fix your family contribution at admission for all four years, and some don't hear appeals (for example Grinnell).
6. **Need-aware means real competition.** At the 51 group-B schools, a large aid request can lower your chance of admission. Only the 10 group-A schools ignore it.

## How this was verified

1. **Started from** William LeBeau's list of 71 schools (see [lebeau-list-audit.md](lebeau-list-audit.md)).
2. **Built the candidate list from** several sources:
   - Money.com's 75 colleges that meet full need (Peterson's data; page last modified 2024)
   - Wikipedia's *Need-blind admission* article
   - Interstride (2023)
   - UniApp's full-need-for-internationals list (sources read up to 18 Nov 2026)
   - Oriel Admissions' Fall 2027 need-blind/need-aware list (May 2026)
   - other known full-need candidates
3. **Checked every school against its own website**, using searches limited to the school's domain. For disputed cases I read the official pages directly: Emory, NYU, Kenyon, Union, Scripps, Skidmore, Soka, Harvey Mudd, Washington and Lee, Lehigh, Thomas Aquinas, Notre Dame, Carleton, Colorado College, Minerva, Curtis, WashU, Middlebury, Case Western and Rhodes.
4. **Searched for 2025–26 policy changes**, including new need-blind announcements and international aid cuts. I found no newer changes. There were also no reports of schools cutting international aid because of the 2026 endowment tax.

**Could not be read:** Crimson Education's list (rate-limited), a counsellor list on sites.google.com (404), and Appily's list (403). These were only used as cross-checks.

**Confidence** is given per school in the CSV:

- **High (88):** an official page makes an explicit statement about international students.
- **Medium (11):** the statement is general, the page is older, or the school's pages disagree.

**Re-check before relying on these Medium-confidence schools:** Case Western, Colorado College, Skidmore, Rhodes, Kenyon, Scripps, Curtis, Olin, Sewanee, Miami and Earlham.

Aid policies change every year. Before applying, re-check each school's international aid page for the current cycle.
