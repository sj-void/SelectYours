01

The big idea

How US colleges can make study affordable for you.

Meets 100% of demonstrated need full need

If you're admitted, the college promises to cover the gap between what it costs and what your family can pay, as the college itself calculates it.

Why it matters
This is what makes a US degree possible for families who could never pay $90,000 a year. All 62 schools on SelectYours make this promise to international students, according to their own official pages G13.
Watch out
  • Full need doesn't mean free. You still pay the family contribution the school calculates.
  • It doesn't always mean no loans. Some schools fill part of the gap with a loan or a campus job G13.
  • Each school uses its own formula, so two full-need schools can ask the same family for very different amounts.

Demonstrated need

The cost of attendance minus what the school's formula says your family can pay.

Why it matters
The school sets both numbers G13. Your "need" is the school's judgement, not your own estimate. That's why the aid forms and documents (section 4) matter so much.

Cost of attendance COA

The school's full budget for one year: tuition, fees, housing, food, insurance, books, personal costs and sometimes travel.

Why it matters
It's the "cost" in the need formula, not what an aided student pays. If you get aid, a higher cost of attendance doesn't make the school more expensive for you, because the school fills the gap.
In SelectYours
The Cost of attendance 2026–27 column. It's blank where a school publishes only its billed charges; the profile then gives those charges line by line. Check whether travel and health insurance are included.
See costs in Explore →

Need-blind vs need-aware admission

Whether asking for financial aid can affect your chance of being admitted.

Need-blind (10 schools)Your aid request isn't considered when the school decides whether to admit you. Admitted students get full need met.
Need-aware (51 schools)The school has a limited aid budget for internationals, so a large aid request can make admission harder. If you're admitted, it still meets full need G13.
How to use it
Don't avoid need-aware schools: they are most of the list. Don't build a list of only need-aware reaches, though. And never under-report your need to look "cheaper". Most of these schools say an international student who doesn't ask for aid at the start can't get it later G13. Berea College has a different model: it covers the full cost for every international student it admits.
Show need-blind schools →

No-loan aid

Some schools meet your need entirely with grants and campus work, with no loans.

Why it matters
Loans are money you repay after graduating, and they're hard to manage if you go home to a lower-paid job market.
In SelectYours
States no loans in aid is "Yes" only where the school's aid policy explicitly says so. "Not stated" means we found no such statement, not that loans are used. Check the profile's Aid tab.
Show no-loan schools →

University vs liberal arts college

Universities have graduate and professional schools. Liberal arts colleges mainly teach undergraduates.

Why it matters
Liberal arts colleges usually mean small classes taught by professors, but fewer specialised majors (check for engineering). They're also mostly left out of global rankings such as QS and THE G12. Universities offer more majors and research labs.
In SelectYours
The Type filter. Compare Undergraduates with Total students to see how many graduate students share the campus.
02

Applying

Platforms, rounds, tests, English and documents.

Application rounds ED · ED II · EA · REA · RD

When you apply, and how much you commit.

ED / ED IIEarly Decision. Binding: if admitted, you attend.
EAEarly Action. An early answer, not binding.
REARestrictive Early Action. Not binding, but limits your other early applications.
RDRegular Decision. The usual round, with answers in spring.
Why it matters
Early rounds can have higher admit rates. But ED commits you before you can compare aid offers, so for an aid applicant it's only safe once you understand the school's aid formula. Some schools make most international aid offers in Regular Decision; Wesleyan and Carleton are examples G13.
In SelectYours
The Application rounds filter shows which rounds each school offers for Fall 2027. The Early Decision admit rate column shows ED admits ÷ ED applicants where the school publishes them (CDS item C21).
See rounds in Explore →

Common App, Coalition, QuestBridge

Platforms that send one application to many colleges, plus each college's own questions.

Good to know
QuestBridge's National College Match is open to students at US high schools and to US citizens and permanent residents abroad, so most international students can't use it G11. Each profile's Admissions tab lists the platforms the school accepts.

Application fee and waivers

Most schools charge a fee per application; most also offer waivers.

In SelectYours
The Application fee column, where the school states it. Fees add up across ten or more schools. The profile lists the waiver routes, which usually go through the Common App or your counsellor.

Testing policy SAT / ACT

Whether the SAT or ACT is required, optional or not considered (test-free) for Fall 2027 entry.

Watch out
"Optional" doesn't always mean optional for everyone. Some schools ask internationals for scores or national exam results instead. The Admissions tab of each profile has the exact rule.
In SelectYours
The Testing, Fall 2027 filter: Required, Optional or Test-free. Hover a value to see the school's wording.
Show test-optional schools →

SAT and ACT middle 50% CDS C9

The middle half of enrolled students' scores: a quarter scored at or below the 25th percentile, and a quarter at or above the 75th.

How to use it
At or above the 75th percentile, your score helps. Below the 25th at a test-optional school, think about not sending it. Where few students submit (see Enrolled who sent the SAT), the range describes only those who did.

English proficiency tests TOEFL · IELTS · Duolingo

Proof of English for applicants whose first language isn't English, unless they qualify for a waiver.

Watch out
Waiver rules differ: "taught in English" can mean the last two years, the last four, or all of school. Where a school gives both a minimum and a recommended score, aim for the recommended one.
In SelectYours
Accepts Duolingo English Test and TOEFL score cited (the minimum or recommended score as the school states it). Both come from the profile's How to apply section.

International documents

Transcripts and translations, predicted grades, and national exam results (IB, A-levels, CBSE, ISC and others).

Tip
Ask your school early how it sends transcripts and predicted grades. If your board exams come after the deadline, check how and when each school wants the results.
03

Admission numbers

What the selectivity figures really tell you.

Common Data Set CDS and IPEDS

The Common Data Set is a standard questionnaire colleges fill in and publish every year. Each item has a code, such as C1 for admissions or H6 for aid to international students G7. IPEDS is the US Department of Education's college data, which we read through College Scorecard.

In SelectYours
Hover any value to see where it came from, for example "CDS 2025–26 C1". "Our calculation" means we divided two published numbers. "re-check" means the source PDF didn't extract cleanly, so confirm the figure in the linked source.

Admit rate CDS C1

Admitted ÷ applicants, for all first-year applicants: domestic and international, every round.

How to use it
The best single measure of selectivity, but not your chance. Below 10%, more than nine in ten applicants are turned down. A balanced list includes schools above about 20–25%. They still meet full need if they admit you.
Show schools above 15% →

International admit rate

The admit rate for nonresident (international) applicants only, where the school publishes it.

Why it matters
Where schools publish both rates, the international rate is usually several times lower than the overall rate. If you'll ask for aid, treat this as your starting point.
Watch out
Most schools don't publish it, so a missing value doesn't mean the gap is small.

Yield

Enrolled ÷ admitted: the share of admitted students who choose the school.

How to read it
High yield means admitted students mostly say yes, which is a sign of strong demand. Schools with high yield can admit fewer students. SelectYours calculates it from CDS item C1.

Class of 2030 figures

Newer admit rates for students who entered in Fall 2026, from the school's own announcement.

Watch out
They're more current than the CDS, but announcements may count differently. Compare them with the CDS figure rather than replacing it.
04

Paying for college

Forms, aid packages and the numbers that show how generous a school is.

CSS Profile, ISFAA, Certification of Finances

The forms schools use to work out your need.

What they are
  • CSS Profile: the College Board's online application for non-federal aid G6.
  • ISFAA: a free alternative that some schools accept.
  • Certification of Finances: shows how you'll pay the part aid doesn't cover.
Tip
Not every college accepts the CSS Profile from internationals G6. Several schools on our list accept a free alternative G13. Gather income documents and translations before November.

What an aid package contains

Grants (free money), loans (repaid later), a campus job, and sometimes summer savings.

How to compare offers
What you really pay each year = family contribution + loans + work expectation + summer savings. F-1 rules limit on-campus work to 20 hours a week while school is in session G1, so a high work expectation takes a lot of your week.

Internationals on aid, average award, aided seats CDS H6

How many international undergraduates got aid from the school, the average award and the total. It covers all four years, not one class.

Why this may be the most useful number
Divide the count by four to get roughly how many aided places there are in each entering class. SelectYours shows this as Aided international seats / class. A school with 40 aided internationals has about 10 aided places a year, shared among applicants from every country.
Generosity check
Avg award ÷ cost compares the average award with the cost of attendance. When the two are close, aided students pay very little.
Show schools with 100+ internationals on aid →

Aid rules that bite later

Renewal, outside scholarships, applying for aid after admission, and transfers.

Watch out
Many schools fix your family's share at admission. Most give little or no aid to international transfer students G13. Your first-year application is usually your one chance at aid. The rules for each school are in its Aid & cost tab.

Endowment per student

The school's invested wealth divided by its number of students.

Why it matters
A rough signal of resources, and of whether a school can keep funding international aid. It's part of our Prestige Index, at 15% G12.
05

Visa and work

The F-1 student visa and what it lets you do.

Form I-20

The "Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status". You need it to apply for an F-1 visa G4.

How it works
After you're accepted, your school's Designated School Official (DSO) issues it G4. Schools usually want your final aid award or proof of funds first. A late aid decision can mean a late visa appointment.

On-campus jobs

F-1 students may work on campus, up to 20 hours a week while school is in session G1.

Watch out
Off-campus work outside the programs below is tightly restricted G1. Plan any job in your aid package within these limits.

CPT Curricular Practical Training

Work authorization for internships that are part of your program, approved by your school.

Watch out
Each school applies CPT differently, and it has knock-on effects. Amherst's page, for example, notes that 12 months or more of full-time CPT removes post-completion OPT. The Life & campus tab of each profile has the school's rules.

OPT Optional Practical Training

Permission to work in the US in your field, most often after you graduate. DHS describes the first period as "often for 12 months" G2.

STEM OPT extension

Graduates of eligible STEM degrees can apply for a further 24-month extension of OPT G2.

Why it matters
If you hope to work in the US after graduating, it can be the most important academic fact. Eligibility depends on your degree's CIP code being on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List G3. The same subject can be STEM-coded at one school and not at another, so check the school's list (in its Academics tab) and the CIP code on your I-20.

Life and place

Where you'll live for four years.

Setting IPEDS locale

A federal classification of the area around campus G5.

Citylarge (250k+), midsize or small principal city
Suburboutside a principal city, inside an urban area
Townfringe ≤10 mi, distant ≤35 mi, remote >35 mi from an urban area
Ruralfringe, distant or remote from an urban area
How to use it
Remote towns and rural campuses mean campus-centred life, long trips to airports, and the need to check break housing. Cities mean internships during term, more food and faith options, and higher living costs.
See place and climate →

Climate NOAA 1991–2020 normals

Thirty-year averages from the weather station nearest campus G9.

In SelectYours
Shown in °C and cm, converted from NOAA's °F and inches; hover for the originals. A winter average low near −10°C, or more than a metre of snow, means serious winter clothing, which is a first-year cost.
Show mild winters →

International students on campus CDS B2

The number and share of degree-seeking undergraduates who are nonresidents.

How to read it
A larger share usually means more support and peers going through the same paperwork. It counts full-paying students too, so for aid use the H6 numbers.

Size CDS B1

Undergraduates, and all students including graduate students, on the CDS count date.

How to read it
At 2,000 students you'll know many classmates, but there are fewer clubs and course options. At 15,000 there's more choice and more anonymity. Neither is better.

Campus safety

Each profile links the school's Annual Security Report, which includes campus crime statistics. The US Department of Education publishes the same data for every school G10.

How to read it
Compare figures per student and in context. A big city campus will always report more than a small rural one, and more reports can also mean reporting works.

Health insurance

Most schools require it and offer their own plan, sometimes costing thousands of dollars a year.

Watch out
Waivers usually need a US-compliant plan. At Amherst, for example, a waiver requires an ACA-compliant plan with providers near campus. Check each school's Life & campus tab, and whether aid covers the plan.
07

Outcomes

Do students finish, and what comes next?

First-year retention CDS B22

The share of first-year students who return for their second year.

How to read it
A rough guide to whether students are happy and supported. All of these schools are high, so small differences mean little.

Graduation rates

The share of an entering class that finishes within 4 or 6 years. Completion rate, internationals is the federal 6-year figure for nonresident students.

Why the 4-year rate matters to you
Aid is usually planned around four years, and an extra term costs money. A big gap between the 4- and 6-year rates is worth asking about.

Median earnings, 10 years

Federal data on former students' earnings, ten years after entering.

Big caveat
It covers only students who received US federal aid, so international students aren't included G8. It also reflects each school's mix of majors. Treat it as broad context only.

Student–faculty ratio CDS I2

Students per full-time-equivalent teacher.

Better signal
Class-size counts (CDS I3, in the Academics tab) are more direct, because they count actual class sections.

Most common fields of study

The share of bachelor's degrees in each broad field, from federal completions data (IPEDS via College Scorecard).

How to use it
It shows what students actually study, which can differ from how a school markets itself. A large share means more courses, faculty and peers in that subject. The Explore filter lets you require, for example, computer science at 10% or more of degrees.

Our scores

Two indexes we compute, kept separate on purpose.

Prestige Index 0–100

How highly the degree is regarded in the US and worldwide.

Tiers
Tier 1, world-renowned (70+); Tier 2, elite (55–69.9); Tier 3, highly regarded (40–54.9); Tier 4, respected (below 40) G12.
How to use it
Look at the parts, not just the total. Liberal arts colleges score 0 on the global part because global rankings mostly leave them out. That reflects how well known they are abroad, not their teaching.

How hard admission is for an international student who needs aid.

60%Admit rate
20%SAT midpoint

10%Few aided seats
Tiers

How to use it
Balance your list: a few Extreme, more Very high and High, and some Competitive.

Published rankings

U.S. News 2027, Forbes 2027, QS 2027 and THE 2027 ranks, as published.

Good to know
U.S. News ranks universities and liberal arts colleges on separate lists, so a #5 college and a #5 university are on different scales. For a range such as "201–250", we show the band and sort by its midpoint.

Distinctions

Ivy League, Ivy-Plus, R1 research and AAU membership, the UK HPI visa list, and top-25 for PhDs per graduate. Shown, but not scored.

Why the UK HPI list matters
The UK's High Potential Individual visa accepts graduates of universities on its global list. If you might want to work in the UK, being on that list has a direct effect G14.

Version 1.1 change: WSJ/College Pulse 2026 is no longer scored. Only its top 100 is public (the full list is paywalled), so 33 of our 62 schools have no WSJ rank, and the WSJ partnership has ended, so no 2027 edition is coming. Pillar A is now 60% U.S. News 2027 + 40% Forbes 2027. The WSJ rank is still shown for schools in its top 100. The tables below describe version 1; read the WSJ weight as moved to these two.

Data note (v1.1):

  • The admit rate, SAT and enrollment come from each school's CDS 2025–26 (Fall 2025), or its official Class of 2030 release, where these could be read.
  • Where a CDS was blocked, unpublished, or only an interactive dashboard, the same figures come from federal IPEDS data for Fall 2024, via the College Scorecard API. For those schools the SAT midpoint is the sum of the section midpoints. This applied to about half the list, and each profile labels its year and source.
  • Where only ACT ranges exist, the SAT midpoint is converted with the official 2018 ACT/SAT concordance (act.org).
  • Six endowments (Wellesley, Mount Holyoke, Berea, Pitzer, Franklin & Marshall, Denison) use the latest published value, June 2024 or approximate, because FY2025 figures were not found. Each is labelled in universities/_data/_endowments.json.

Applies to: the 62 schools in us-full-need-schools-international.md. Audience: international students who need full financial aid.

Every source is listed at the bottom, numbered [n].

1. Principles

  • No single ranking covers all 62 schools fairly.
    • 24 of the 62 are universities and 38 are liberal arts colleges (LACs), using the classification in our CSV.
    • U.S. News ranks those two groups on separate lists 1.
    • QS only ranks institutions that offer both undergraduate and postgraduate degrees 2.
    • THE excludes institutions with fewer than 1,000 publications over five years 3.
    • Only 17 of the 62 are on the UK's list of globally top-ranked universities 13.
  • Rankings feed on themselves and depend on self-reported data. Published ranks shape later reputation scores 10, and Columbia's misreported data got it removed from the 2023 U.S. News edition 11. So every component is shown, never just a final number.
  • Prestige and difficulty are kept separate. A school can be prestigious but easier to get into, and students should be able to see that.
  • Fixed formulas and tier cut-offs were set before any school was scored, so no result could be tuned after the fact.
  • Missing data: if a school has no value for a component, that component's weight is spread proportionally over the school's other components, and the school is flagged "partial data".

Why these sources:

  • U.S. News is the only US ranking with a direct reputation survey: 20% of its score comes from ratings by presidents, provosts and deans 1.
  • Forbes puts universities and LACs on a single list and rewards alumni leadership (15%) and fellowships and PhDs (10%) 4.
  • WSJ is mostly about value added, with 70% of its score from outcomes, so it gets the smallest share of pillar A 5.
  • QS, THE and ARWU measure how the name is known worldwide:
    • QS surveys 151,000 academics and 100,000 employers 2.
    • THE's reputation measures (33%) come from 69,000+ responses 3.
    • ARWU counts Nobel and Fields winners, highly cited researchers and Nature/Science papers 8.
    • Governments use these rankings: the UK's High Potential Individual visa accepts only graduates of universities in the top 100 of at least two of the three 1314.
  • Endowment per student measures real resources, and also signals whether a school can keep funding international aid.

Turning ranks into points:

  • National ranks: 100 × (1 − √((rank − 1) / 249)), with a minimum of 0. Roughly: #1 = 100, #10 = 81, #50 = 56, #100 = 23, #250 and below = 0.
  • Global ranks: 100 × (1 − √((rank − 1) / 399)). Roughly: #1 = 100, #50 = 65, #150 = 39, #400 and below = 0.
  • For a range such as "201–250", the midpoint is used.
  • Wealth uses a log scale with fixed ends: 100 × ln(per-student endowment / $100,000) / ln(30), limited to 0–100. So $100k = 0 and $3M = 100.

5. Changes from the first draft, and why

  1. Dropped the scholarly-pipeline pillar from the score. Institution-level NSF figures only exist for the top 25 to 50 schools, so two-thirds of the list would have been scored on missing data.
  2. Pillar A weights: U.S. News 50, Forbes 30, WSJ 20. Only U.S. News has a reputation survey. WSJ measures value added rather than prestige, and its latest edition is a year older.
  3. Wider rank curves (national to #250, global to #400). The draft's #150 cut-off pushed schools ranked around #40–#80, still in the top 10–20% nationally, too low.
  4. Wealth on a fixed log scale instead of a percentile, so scores don't change when schools are added or removed.
  5. Difficulty reweighted (admit rate 60, tests 20, need policy 10, aid seats 10). The draft made generous need-blind schools such as Harvard look easier than they are. The admit rate is both the most reliable number and the main driver of difficulty.
  6. Tier cut-offs fixed before scoring.

How the research is done

Every fact has a source
Every value links to the document it came from, with its data year. Nothing is filled in from memory or estimated. If a fact isn't published, the profile says "Not published on the pages checked" and names the site and date searched.
Source priority
The school's own pages and documents come first, then US federal data (IPEDS, College Scorecard, NOAA), then official bodies (College Board, test providers), then reputable press, labelled as press. We never cite consultancy blogs, forums or Wikipedia, and we never get around a site's bot protection or log-in.
Data year
For applicants in the 2026–27 cycle (Fall 2027 entry). Costs are for 2026–27. Admissions figures are from the CDS 2025–26 or the school's Class of 2030 release.
